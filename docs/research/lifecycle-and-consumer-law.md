# Domain lifecycle timing, renewal and refund rules, and auto-renewal consumer law

Mosshatch Phase 0 research dossier. Access date for every source: **2026-09-29**. Research only; nothing under /home/user/MossHatch was modified.
Evidence tags: **[P]** primary (statute, regulator, ICANN, registry, card-network, standards body, Stripe docs), **[S]** secondary (a registrar's own documentation). Finding IDs (E1, A1, S3 ...) refer to the full findings table in section 9; every quote in that table was machine-checked against the fetched source text (the one exception is L1, read from an ICANN chart image).

## TL;DR

1. ICANN's Expired Registration Recovery Policy (ERRP, updated 21 Feb 2024, mandatory since 21 Aug 2025) requires two pre-expiry notices (about T-30 d and T-7 d; safe harbour 26-35 and 4-10 days), one more notice within 5 days after expiry, DNS interruption for at least the last 8 days before deletion, a 30-day Redemption Grace Period, and publication of renewal, post-expiry-renewal and restore fees, including on reseller sites (so on Mosshatch). It does NOT require the same price during the grace window; that is our product choice.
2. gTLD timeline (ICANN chart/EPP page): 5-day Add Grace Period, auto-renew grace 0-45 d, Redemption 30 d, Pending Delete 5 d, released about day 80 (Gandi: .com/.dev/.app/.studio day 80, .io day 90, .ai day 89 with conflicting pages). .io and .ai are ccTLDs outside ERRP/AGP; the .io registry's own terms say no refund once registered.
3. AGP Limits Policy: a registry refunds a registrar for at most max(10% of net adds, 50) AGP deletes per TLD per month, so upstream refund windows (often shorter than 5 days, e.g. Porkbun 96 h) and per-reseller caps decide what Mosshatch can promise; renewals are not refundable upstream (Gandi: non-payment risk is the reseller's), so charge first, renew upstream second.
4. US: the FTC click-to-cancel amendments were vacated by the 8th Circuit on 8 Jul 2025; the FTC restored the old prenotification-plan rule on 12 Feb 2026 and opened an ANPRM on 13 Mar 2026 (comments closed 13 Apr 2026; no NPRM found). ROSCA plus state laws are what bind us: CA (annual renewals: notice 15-45 d before renewal, applies to every renewal from 1 Jul 2025), VT (opt-in checkbox + notice 30-60 d), NY (notice 15-45 d, consent to price rises; effective 180 days after enactment), MN (1 Jan 2025), VA.
5. EU/UK: 14-day withdrawal right for distance service contracts; lost only after FULL performance with prior express request and acknowledgement; no domain-specific exclusion found and no guidance on whether registration is "fully performed" (unverified - lawyer). EU "withdraw here" button (CRD Art. 11a) applies from 19 Jun 2026; UK DMCCA subscription regime still "Prospective", expected Spring 2027 with a 14-day post-renewal cooling-off.
6. Cards (Visa Core Rules 18 Apr 2026): express informed consent to a stored-credential agreement shown separately from T&Cs, simple online cancel, notice at least 7 days before a recurring charge when a trial/promo ends and, in the Europe Region, for annual renewals, and after a decline written notice plus at least 7 calendar days to pay by other means. Mastercard's rulebook returned 403 (unverified). Hosted Stripe Checkout keeps us at PCI SAQ A (annual attestation, new script-attack eligibility criterion).
7. Recommended policy: auto-renew OFF by default; unchecked box beside the first-year price; passkey-signed mandate with a price cap; charge date C = expiry E minus 10 days; notices at E-45, E-32, E-17 (C-7), charge at C, decline ladder C+0/+3/+6 with 7 days to pay otherwise, post-expiry notice E+1; registrations refundable for min(5 days, upstream window), .io/.ai shown as non-refundable up front; renewal "refund" means the name is deleted (registry credit), unless we charged without a valid mandate.

### 1.2 RAA items that bite a reseller

- RAA 3.7.5 / 3.7.5.3: absent extenuating circumstances, a name whose holder has not consented to renewal after the second notice is cancelled by the end of the auto-renew grace period, and a name must be deleted within 45 days after either side ends the registration agreement (matters for account closure). A registrant's card failing is not among the listed extenuating circumstances (R1, R2, R6).
- RAA 3.7.5.4-3.7.5.6: each new registrant is told the deletion/auto-renew policy including the expected deletion time (or a range of at most 10 days); the policy is displayed on the site; the RGP recovery fee is stated at registration (R3, R4).
- RAA 3.12: the registrar is responsible for its resellers; reseller registration agreements must carry all ICANN-required provisions and name the sponsoring registrar (R5).

### 1.3 Add Grace Period and the AGP Limits Policy

- AGP is "typically the five-day period following the initial registration"; a name deleted in it is immediately available to anyone (A2). Registry credit for such deletes is capped (A1): **max(10% of the registrar's net new registrations, 50) per TLD per month**, unless the operator grants an exemption for extraordinary, non-recurring circumstances (A3, A4).
- Worked thresholds (computed from the policy text): 100 net adds -> 50 refundable deletes; 500 -> 50; 1,000 -> 100; 5,000 -> 500. ICANN's own example: 1,000 net adds and 250 AGP deletes -> refund for 100, the other 150 are the registrar's loss.
- The pool is the sponsoring registrar's, not Mosshatch's. Name.com's API says its `isRefundable` flag "already accounts for ICANN AGP limits, reseller-level thresholds, and name.com refund policies" (L14), so an upstream can impose per-reseller caps. Porkbun promises only 96 hours and charges a 5% refund fee (L13). Mosshatch's public refund window must therefore be min(5 days from registry create time, the upstream's window), gated by the upstream's own eligibility check.
- Purpose is anti-tasting (A4), so Mosshatch needs its own abuse guard (per-account refund count per 30 days).

### 1.6 Why the upstream choice matters here

The customer-facing grace, the DNS cut date, the refund window, the restore cost, and whether renewals can be refunded are all set by the sponsoring registrar, not by ICANN minimums. Selection criteria that fall out of this dossier: documented late-renewal window at unchanged price (>= 30 days), restore via API with published fee, AGP delete/refund via API with an `isRefundable`-style check, DNS behaviour on expiry, no auction of expired names, and a written statement of any per-reseller AGP cap. Cloudflare-style "expire straight into RGP" (L6) would not fit the no-surprises rule.


## 2. Consumer-protection law on auto-renewal and cancellation

Scope note: several statutes protect only "consumers" (individuals buying for personal, family or household purposes). Mosshatch's users are often developers and businesses, but the product should apply the strictest common rule to everyone; whether B2B buyers are outside ROSCA or the state laws was not verified (lawyer item).

What binds Mosshatch today at federal level:
- **ROSCA 15 USC 8403**: no charge through a negative option feature for an online sale unless (1) all material terms are disclosed clearly and conspicuously before billing information is obtained, (2) express informed consent is obtained before charging, and (3) simple mechanisms to stop recurring charges are provided (U1). An opt-in auto-renew is still a "negative option feature" because the renewal continues unless the customer cancels (the ANPRM lists "automatic renewals" as a negative-option category, U4), so the opt-in checkbox is what satisfies prong 2; a domain the customer must actively renew each year is not a negative option.
- **FTC Act s.5** principles restated in the ANPRM: disclose material terms (existence of the offer, total cost, how to cancel), clear and conspicuous, before agreement, obtain consent, do not impede cancellation (U7).
- Design inference (not a quoted rule): ROSCA speaks of the consumer's consent, so an AI agent's scoped token must not be able to grant it; consent is the human passkey approval.

### 2.3 European Union

- **Withdrawal right** (CRD Art. 9): 14 days, no reason (X1). **Exception** (Art. 16(a)): none for a service contract until it "has been fully performed", and then only if performance began with the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgement that the right is lost once fully performed (X2). The Directive text has no domain-name carve-out.
- **Is a domain registration "fully performed" at registration?** Not answered by the Directive or by the Commission's 2021 guidance (which never mentions domain names, X3). A one-year registration is a continuing service until the term ends, and the guidance applies pro-rata-temporis compensation to subscription-type services (X4, CJEU C-641/19). Practical reading (an inference, not a verified holding): an EU consumer can probably withdraw within 14 days and owes at most a proportionate amount, which could exceed the 5-day registry refund window. **Unverified; lawyer.**
- **Express consent means a positive act**: the Commission says a pre-ticked box or T&C clause does not qualify (X3); Art. 22 gives reimbursement of any extra payment inferred from default options (X5). Pre-contract information must include termination conditions for automatically extended contracts (X6).
- **New: withdrawal function** (Directive (EU) 2023/2673, Art. 11a CRD): distance contracts concluded through an online interface need a "withdraw from contract here" function; Member States apply from **19 Jun 2026** (X7). Per-state transposition not checked. The consolidated CRD text on EUR-Lex (28.05.2022) predates it.
- National add-ons exist, e.g. Germany BGB 312k "Verträge hier kündigen" button (X8). Digital Fairness Act status: not verified.

### 2.4 United Kingdom

- Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013: 14-day cancellation period for services (K1); right lost only if the service is fully performed after an express request and with acknowledgement (reg. 36(2)), otherwise a proportionate payment (reg. 36(4)-(5)) (K2); no additional payment on a pre-ticked box (reg. 40) (K3). legislation.gov.uk states it is up to date to 29 Sep 2026.
- **DMCCA 2024 subscription regime**: s.254 and following are marked "Prospective" as of 29 Sep 2026 (K5). Government (2 Apr 2026): rules "expected to be in Spring 2027", with reminders before 12-month+ renewals and a **14-day cooling-off after a 12-month+ contract auto-renews**, full or proportionate refund (K4). Schedule 22 (excluded contracts) has no entry for domains or hosting. Our annual auto-renewals will fall inside it.

### 3.3 Stripe [P]

- Saving a card for later off-session use requires terms covering the customer's agreement to your initiating payments, timing/frequency, how the amount is determined and the cancellation policy, plus a kept record; "if you want to automatically charge a saved payment method for future subscription renewals, you must first get explicit consent for that" - Stripe's own example is a "Save my payment method for automatic renewals" checkbox (C7).
- **Checkout gotcha**: with hosted Checkout, `payment_intent_data.setup_future_usage` saves the card even if the customer ignores Checkout's optional save checkbox; that checkbox (`saved_payment_method_options.payment_method_save`) only controls prefill/redisplay (C10). So Mosshatch must collect the auto-renew opt-in on its own page and set `setup_future_usage=off_session` only when it is ticked. Otherwise a card is saved for a customer who never opted in.
- Saved off-session cards are marked merchant-initiated (MIT) and can be SCA-exempt only if authenticated when saved (`setup_future_usage=off_session` / SetupIntent `usage=off_session`); exemptions are not guaranteed and the customer may have to come back on-session (C9, and Stripe SCA doc). A card-brand change from Card Account Updater blocks MITs until a new agreement is obtained (C8).
- Refunds do not return Stripe's fees (C11); customers see refunds after roughly 5-10 business days.

### 3.4 PCI DSS with Stripe-hosted Checkout [P]

- Stripe: Checkout/Elements qualify for **SAQ A** because card inputs are hosted on Stripe's domain; businesses still "must ... annually attest" (C12, C14).
- PCI SSC (30 Jan 2025): the SAQ A effective 31 Mar 2025 removes requirements 6.4.3, 11.6.1 and 12.3.1 but adds an eligibility criterion to confirm the site "is not susceptible to attacks from scripts"; validation obligations come from acquirers/brands, not the Council (C13). PCI DSS v4.0.1 is the standard featured in the Council's document library on the access date. With a pure redirect to Checkout, the script-attack criterion is easiest to meet; keep third-party scripts off the pages that lead to Checkout.
- Mosshatch may store brand, last four and expiry (non-sensitive, Stripe security guide), which it needs for notices.


## 4. Recommended Mosshatch renewal and refund policy

Notation: E = the domain's expiry date; C = the auto-renew charge date = **E minus 10 days**; "T" offsets are calendar days. Worked example: E = 2027-03-15, C = 2027-03-05. This is a proposal built from the verified rules above. The offsets E-45, E-32, E-17, C = E-10, the retry days, the 24 h upstream retry and the abuse-guard N are Mosshatch choices picked to sit inside the verified windows; anything cited to a finding ID, statute or policy is a rule.

### 4.1 Consent: opt-in only, no negative option by default

1. **Default is reminder mode**: no card is stored for renewals, and each renewal is a one-tap passkey-approved payment (a customer-initiated transaction, so no negative option and no Visa 13.2 exposure). This is legally the simplest path.
2. **Auto-renew is an opt-in upgrade**: an unchecked box on Mosshatch's own page, in the same block as the first-year price, reading in effect "Year 1: $X. Renews on [C] at $Y per [term] unless you turn this off. Charged to card ending 4242." It is separate from the ToS box (Vermont, Visa Table 5-20).
3. **The passkey approves the mandate, not each charge.** The signed record stores: domain, renewal term, price cap, text-version hash, timestamp, IP/user agent, WebAuthn assertion id, and the Stripe payment method id. Retain >= 3 years after the last renewal or 1 year after termination, whichever is longer (California).
4. Stripe: create the Checkout Session with `payment_intent_data.setup_future_usage=off_session` only when the box is ticked (section 3.3). Do not rely on Checkout's own save checkbox.
5. Agents: a scoped agent token may read renewal state, turn auto-renew OFF, and lower the cap; it may not turn it ON, raise the cap, change the term, or pay. Turning ON or raising the price cap requires a fresh passkey approval. Switching OFF never requires a passkey (cancellation must be at least as easy as sign-up).
6. Renewal term = exactly the disclosed term (1 year, or the TLD minimum if the upstream forces 2 years for .ai - unverified). Gandi's own auto-renew repeats the last purchased period, which is the kind of surprise to avoid.

### 4.3 How off-session renewals are charged, and what happens when the card fails

Charge flow (all choices except where noted):
1. At C, create a PaymentIntent for the saved payment method with `off_session=true`, `confirm=true`, an idempotency key `(domain, term_start)`, and the mandate id in metadata.
2. **Only after `payment_intent.succeeded`, call the upstream renew endpoint.** Upstream renewals are not refundable and non-payment risk is the reseller's (Gandi FAQ, L12).
3. If the upstream call fails after payment: retry with backoff for 24 h, then refund in full automatically and page an operator. The domain is not at risk at C because E is 10 days away.
4. Send the receipt immediately (California acknowledgement).

Failure ladder:
- **Decline or `authentication_required`**: send the written notice at once with a pay-now link (on-session, passkey-approved, 3-D Secure if needed). Visa requires at least 7 calendar days to pay by other means (C4); Stripe says to bring the customer back on-session (C9).
- **Retries**: at C+3 and C+6 for soft declines only; never retry a hard decline (lost/stolen/closed account); no more than 3 attempts. No late fees.
- **Card brand change or expiry**: card-updater events move the mandate to "needs re-approval" (C8).
- **After C+7**: auto-renew is suspended, the domain stays fully live until E, then follows the expiry ladder below at the unchanged price. The customer is never charged again without a new passkey approval.
- Why C = E-10 (choice): with 7 days to pay plus two retries, the ladder ends 3 days before expiry, so a failed card never causes DNS loss by itself; and the ERRP one-week window (E-10..E-4) is covered by the failure notices.

### 4.4 Expiry ladder after E (depends on the upstream; ERRP gives only the floor)

Late renewal at the unchanged renewal price for the whole upstream window (>= 30 days is a selection criterion); restore during the 30-day RGP at the published restore fee (upstream fee + flat fee, shown on the fee page and at registration); pending delete is final; released names are gone. Show the customer a timeline with exact dates per domain. No auction, parking or monetisation of expired names.

### 4.5 Refund policy (consistent with the 5-day AGP)

| Case | Policy | Why |

Name is deleted and immediately released. | AGP is typically 5 days (A2); upstream windows can be shorter (Porkbun 96 h, L13); cap and per-reseller limits (A1, L14). |

### 4.6 Edge cases

- **Opt-in too close to the charge date**: if C is less than 35 days away when the customer ticks the box, do not auto-charge this cycle; offer "renew now" (a passkey-approved one-time payment) and enrol the mandate for the following cycle. Whether the opt-in confirmation itself can serve as the notice is a lawyer question.
- **Multi-year terms and .ai**: the notice shows the actual term and amount; notices for renewals longer than 12 months also satisfy Virginia's 30-60 day rule.
- **Price rise above the cap**: hold the charge and ask for passkey re-approval (NY: consent to a price above the disclosed one, or a 14-day cancel with pro-rata refund).
- **Account closure**: offer transfer-out; otherwise delete within 45 days (RAA 3.7.5.3).
- **Chargebacks**: 120-day Visa 13.2 window; keep the mandate, notices and cancellation log as evidence.
- **Notices go to the registrant, not only to the domain's mailbox**, because an expired domain's email may stop working (ERRP best practice).

## 6. Design implications

1. Default to reminder mode (no stored card, one-tap passkey-approved renewal). Auto-renew is an opt-in upgrade: unchecked box next to the first-year price, separate from ToS, approved by passkey as a mandate with a price cap; set Stripe setup_future_usage=off_session ONLY when the box is ticked, because Checkout saves the card anyway once that parameter is set.
2. Publish a fee page (renewal, late renewal at the same price, restore fee, refund windows per TLD) and a notification-methods page; link both from checkout and the registration agreement. ERRP 4.1.2/4.2.3 makes reseller display mandatory.
3. Use the upstream registrar only if it documents: late-renewal window at unchanged price (>= 30 days), restore via API with a published fee, AGP delete/refund via API with an isRefundable-style check, DNS behaviour on expiry, no auction of expired names, per-reseller AGP caps. Cloudflare-style straight-to-RGP expiry does not fit the no-surprises rule.
4. Charge first, renew upstream second (upstream renewals are non-refundable and non-payment risk is the reseller's). Charge date C = expiry E - 10 days.
5. Notice schedule: E-45 (renewal notice, satisfies CA/NY/VT/VA), E-32 and E-7 (ERRP, all unrenewed domains), C-21 (price change), C-7 (Visa pre-charge), E+1 (ERRP post-expiry). Keep each notice inside its legal window with a margin of at least 2 days (35 days for a 30-45 day window, 32 days for a 26-35 day window).
6. Failure ladder: written decline notice immediately, at least 7 calendar days to pay by other means (Visa), retries at C+3 and C+6 for soft declines only, then suspend auto-renew (not delete) and require a passkey to resume; never let a failed card alone cause DNS loss.
7. Refund window = min(5 days from registry create time, upstream window), gated by the upstream's eligibility check, with a per-account abuse guard. Show the exact cut-off timestamp at checkout. Show .io/.ai as non-refundable unless the upstream says otherwise.
8. A renewal 'refund' means deleting the name (registry credit); the UI must say so. If Mosshatch charged without a valid mandate, refund in full and keep the domain at Mosshatch's cost.
9. Data model: mandate (text hash, cap, term, passkey assertion id, timestamps), notice log (type, template version, sent_at, channel), cancellation log, refund log; retain >= 3 years after last renewal or 1 year after termination.
10. Agents may switch auto-renew off and lower the cap; only a human passkey may switch it on, raise the cap, change the term or pay. Switching off never needs a passkey.
11. Per-TLD lifecycle and refund panel before purchase: .io/.ai are ccTLDs outside ERRP/AGP with different pending-delete lengths and refund rules; .ai may force 2-year renewals (unverified), which must appear in notices.
12. PCI: redirect-only Stripe Checkout, SAQ A annually, no card fields or third-party scripts on pages leading to Checkout (CSP/SRI) to meet the new script-attack eligibility criterion.
13. EU/UK: unticked express-request-and-acknowledgement checkbox for immediate performance, dashboard 'withdraw from contract here' function (EU from 19 Jun 2026), UK 14-day post-renewal cooling-off ready for Spring 2027.
14. Listen for Stripe card-updater brand changes and authentication_required declines; both move the mandate to 'needs re-approval' and trigger an on-session email.
15. The expired-state page (sleeping creature) must state that the name is expired and give renewal instructions; ERRP requires this for registrar-directed pages, and DNS interruption itself is done by the upstream.
