# reg-centralnic - adversarial verification, LEGAL / PROGRAM lens

Verifier run 2026-09-29. Every source below was re-fetched by me (curl -sSL, browser UA, via the pre-configured proxy; no TLS bypass; no egress blocks hit; WebSearch budget was already exhausted so no search was used). Raw pages are in `.../research/vlegal/raw/` (independent of the analyst's `cn/` folder). I did not reuse any of the analyst's saved text. All quotes in the dossier that I tested (49 exact-substring checks against my own fetches of MSA, Registration Agreement, T&C, Anti-Abuse Policy, FAQ, program page, pricing page, sign-up form, NIS2 and sanctions KB pages) matched verbatim. The problems are in interpretation, omissions and three factual slips, listed below.

## Bottom line

The core legal claim holds: an independent company may resell under its own brand, price and checkout, ordering via API, under the Key-Systems GmbH Wholesale MSA + Reseller Schedule. No "own use only" API clause, no resale ban that survives the Reseller Schedule, no separate API terms exist (OpenAPI `termsOfService` = https://www.rrpproxy.net/Legal/Terms_und_Conditions redirects to the CNR home page (final URL https://www.centralnicreseller.com/, 1 redirect); the API is defined in MSA 1.1/1.25 and governed by the MSA). So "resale = yes" is CONFIRMED, with caveats.

What the dossier gets wrong or under-states (details in claim table):
1. REFUTED: "Default renewal mode = automatic deletion". KB and MSA both say the default is AutoRenew.
2. CORRECTED: the "100 domains vs no minimum" is not a contradiction between two pages; both sentences sit in the same pricing-page FAQ answer, and neither is in the MSA (which has an entire-agreement clause).
3. CORRECTED: termination summary (both 13.1 limbs start at month 36; 15-day-cure breach termination and the 13.7 transfer-out duty were omitted).
4. CORRECTED: "records 2 years" (it is Term + 2 years, and evidence must be kept "as long as legally possible" / "life-time of the Registration").
5. CORRECTED/UNRESOLVED: B2B/EU-consumer attestation is ambiguous and its headline sentence is broader than the dossier's reading. This may be a program blocker for EU individual registrants.
6. Omitted entirely: Data Processing Agreement, Reseller Schedule 3.6 (Mosshatch liable for all fees whether or not it collects), 3.1-3.3 (must flow the whole MSA down into Mosshatch's own terms), MSA 13.7, 23, 28, 31, promo activation conditions, ICANN website-disclosure duties.

## Claim-by-claim verdicts

### 1. Resale to end customers under own brand is permitted - CONFIRMED (with caveats)
- MSA 5.2 verbatim as quoted; Reseller Schedule 1 verbatim ("Subject to the Customers' compliance with the terms and conditions of this Schedule and acceptance of all applicable Schedules, Appendices and Third-Party Policies, Service Provider grants permission ..."). The grant is conditional; the dossier's quote drops the condition.
- R&M Schedule 1.2.1: "Customer is prohibited from engaging in Reseller Activity with regard to domain names unless explicitly agreed with the Service provider in writing." 1.2.2 then says agreement to the Reseller Schedule "shall be considered sufficient agreement". The dossier quotes only 1.2.2. Caveat: MSA 1.23 defines "Schedule" as "a written document, signed by the Parties", and the FAQ says the RSP General Agreement etc. must be signed for a "verified" account. The public sign-up only ticks T&C, MSA and Registration Agreement (SIGN). Whether an unverified, unsigned account already holds the resale permission is not stated anywhere. Get it in writing.
- MSA 5.3(iv) forbids reselling "Customer's rights granted in this Agreement" except as expressly permitted; the Reseller Schedule is that express permission. MSA 4: Access Data "limited to use by the Customer and Users" (not transferable) - fine for a server-side integration, but do not hand API credentials to end customers.
- MSA 5.4 (first one): "bulk registration attempts of deleted domains names" may be classed as abusive, and SP may "temporarily disable Customers' access to the System". Relevant to high-volume availability/drop-style automation. The dossier cites 5.4 only for access disabling.

### 2. Own price, own checkout, "white label" - CONFIRMED (quotes), with a contract-vs-marketing conflict
- PROG quotes verbatim ("sell domains to the public under their own brand, without ICANN accreditation. You control the pricing and customer relationships ..." ; "Customers purchase through your branded site while registrations are automated via CentralNic Reseller."). PRICE FAQ verbatim ("You set the domain reseller pricing on your own storefront ...").
- Conflict the dossier only half flags: PROG FAQ says a white-label reseller sells "with no mention of the underlying registrar"; PRICE FAQ says "no CentralNic Reseller branding visible". The contract says the opposite in three places: Reseller Sched. 4.3 (registrants must enter a Registration Agreement with Key-Systems GmbH and Mosshatch must identify Key-Systems as sponsoring registrar on inquiry), ICANN RAA 3.12.2-3.12.3 (reseller registration agreement must identify the sponsoring registrar; verified in the archived 2013 RAA text), and Reseller Sched. 3.7 (SP may contact your customers). MSA 25 (no use of the other party's name in publicity without permission) cuts the other way. Treat "no visible CNR/Key-Systems" as marketing, not a contractual right.
- Reseller Sched. 5.2 adds: Mosshatch "shall not represent or imply to any Customer-Client that Customer has any authority to act on behalf of or to represent Service Provider."

### 3. Registrant of record is the end customer; Mosshatch must obtain and evidence acceptance of the Key-Systems Registration Agreement - CONFIRMED
- Reseller Sched. 4.4 verbatim (note it ends "unless expressly agreed otherwise with the Registrant"); 4.3 verbatim; RA s.1 verbatim ("Customers who act as resellers or distributors are obliged to transmit these conditions completely and obligatorily to the end customer/registrants and to document their approval by appropriate evidence"). R&M 1.3.6: registrant "shall be considered the legal title holder".
- ICANN RAA 3.12 (opened myself): registrar must have written agreements with resellers enabling it to perform the RAA, and must ensure resellers (3.12.1) do not show ICANN logo / claim accreditation, (3.12.2) use registration agreements carrying all RAA-required provisions and identifying the sponsoring registrar, (3.12.3) identify the sponsoring registrar on inquiry, (3.12.5) give customers the ICANN registrant education link, (3.12.7) publish the Registrants' Benefits and Responsibilities. Reseller Sched. 4.3, 4.10, 4.11 are the flow-downs. The dossier never cites the RAA; this is the ICANN flow-down the lens asked about.

### 4. Reseller duties: expiry notices, auth codes, ICANN B&R publication, records - CONFIRMED except "records 2 years" (CORRECTED)
- 4.7 (1 month + 1 week before, and within 5 days after expiry) verbatim. Nuance: the post-expiry notice applies only "for Domain Names in TLDs with a renew grace period" and not if renewal was already requested. 4.6 (auth code within 5 days) verbatim. 4.10 verbatim.
- CORRECTED - records: Reseller Sched. 3.10 says records must be kept "during the Term of the Agreement and for two years thereafter" (so the clock runs from the end of the whole contract, not per record), 4.6 says evidence of authorisation must be kept "for as long as legally possible", and R&M 1.3.3 requires supplementary documentary evidence to be kept "for the life-time of the Registration". The dossier headline "2-year records" understates this. Records must also be producible "for inspection and creation of copies upon reasonable request".
- Additional duties not in the dossier: 4.7 first sentence - inform registrants of deletion/renewal procedures and time-frames "(including renewal fees, restore fees and transfer fees) at the time of Registration as well as on its websites"; 4.5 do not activate the domain in DNS until payment is "reasonably assured"; 3.5 notify customers of upcoming renewals and facilitate renewal; 3.7 forward SP notifications to customers.
- ICANN side (KB-ERRP and RAA 3.7.5.4-3.7.5.6, opened): registrars/resellers must "clearly display their renewal and restore prices and a description of the methods used to deliver pre- and post-expiration notifications" on their web sites; the RAA requires the deletion/auto-renewal policy to be shown on the site and the RGP recovery fee stated "both at the time of registration and in a clear place on its website". Not in the dossier.

### 5. NIS2 verification logs; fines chargeable to reseller; live 2025-11-25 - CONFIRMED
KB-NIS2 verbatim on all three quotes and "The updates are available in live environment since 25th November 2025." Also states non-compliance can mean rejected registrations, suspended domains and "In extreme cases, suspension of your account" (omitted by dossier).

### 6. Program requirements: online sign-up, EUR 75, unverified caps, signed RSP contracts, MSA 13.5 background checks - CONFIRMED

The amount of allowed API commands is limited as well, as you are in the Basic price scale."; Account Manager sends "RSP General Agreement, Confidentiality Agreement, RSP Data Info-Form" for "email, fax or post". MSA 13.5 verbatim.

### 7. "100 domains per year qualification conflicts with pricing page 'no minimum'" - CORRECTED
- FAQ verbatim confirms the 100-domain rule ("must register at least 100 domains within 1 year OR transfer at least 100 ... You'll also need API access").
- But the PRICE page FAQ answer is a single answer with two paragraphs: (a) "No, there is no minimum purchase required to access our domain reseller pricelist. You can start with as little as a single domain registration ..." and (b) "Please note that, to qualify as a CentralNic Reseller's customer, you should either register a minimum of 100 domains within one year or transfer at least 100 domains to CentralNic Reseller following sign-up." So the pricing page does not contradict the rule; it distinguishes price-list access (no minimum) from customer qualification (100, worded "should"). The dossier's "the pricing page FAQ says no minimum (conflict)" reads only paragraph (a).
- Legal weight: MSA 26 (entire agreement, supersedes prior communications) and the whole MSA contain no 100-domain commitment; the only usage-based right is 13.1(ii) (terminate if "not using any paid Services", after month 36). The FAQ figure is therefore a marketing/qualification statement; whether the unpublished RSP General Agreement or Account Manager enforces it is unverifiable. Still worth a written waiver.

### 8. Account service fee - CONFIRMED (exists) / amount UNVERIFIABLE
FAQ verbatim: "we charge an account-level service fee. This fee does not apply to all customers." with monthly/yearly schedule under Settings > Paid Services. It is not in the public MSA (14.1 says fees are in Schedules "or published in the respective Customer Portal"). PROG FAQ says "No revenue share, no surprise fees" - in tension with an undisclosed account fee.

### 9. Termination: "6 months' notice after 36 months, or immediately if no paid services are used" - CORRECTED
MSA 13.1 verbatim: "Beginning 36 months after the Effective Date, (i) either party may terminate ... at least six (6) months in advance ..., and (ii) Service Provider shall have the right to immediately terminate the Agreement upon written notice to the Customer in the event the Customer is not using any paid Services." The lead-in governs both limbs, so the "immediately if unused" right also begins at month 36 (the dossier program summary reads as if it applies from day one; the dossier body at 1.2 states it correctly). Omitted from the dossier:
- 13.2: either party may terminate for material breach after a 15-day cure period, and the MSA deems as material breach any "violation of any Service Provider terms or policies or Third-Party Policies", non-payment, and failure to answer urgent SP inquiries within 15 days.
- 13.5: failure of a background-check/validation "to the satisfaction of Service Provider" may be treated as material breach.
- 13.7: on termination Mosshatch must "Transfer all Services ... to another provider prior to the End of the Term", pre-existing orders stop being processed, and SP "shall be relieved of all responsibility for the further (i) maintenance of Services not transferred to another provider". With the ICANN/registry 60-day post-registration lock, and 14.2's two-week exit right on a price rise, a fast exit cannot move recently registered names. This is the main portfolio/exit risk and it is not in the dossier.
- 22 sunsetting: SP "will endeavor to assist" transition; 30 days to terminate.

### 10. Unilateral price change (14.2, RA s.4, T&C 5) - CONFIRMED
14.2 quotes verbatim, including the two-weeks-notice exit. Additional wording: SP may also raise fees where a third party's policy change "increase[s] the cost base of the Service Provider"; for registry increases SP "shall endeavor to provide thirty (30) days' written notice ... or without undue delay" (so no hard minimum). RA s.4 verbatim ("renewal and transfer fees may change on a short notice", "Registrar is authorized to cancel or modify orders if a price change occurs between the date of the order and the fulfillment date"). T&C 5 verbatim ("The prices can be changed at any time."). RA s.2a lets SP undo a premium-name transaction if the premium price was displayed wrongly. PRICE FAQ ("We provide advance notice of any modifications") is a softer marketing statement than 14.2.

### 11. Suspension without notice on low balance; chargeback forfeiture - CONFIRMED; and a larger money-risk clause is missing
14.3 and 14.7 verbatim. T&C 6 adds that SP may "cease all services after unsuccessful reminder without notice and ... block the access of the customer to his account(s)". MISSED by dossier: Reseller Sched. 3.6 "Customer is responsible and liable to Service Provider for all fees for operations or Services requested ... (without regard to Customer's ability to collect fees owed to Customer by Customer-Clients)". Mosshatch carries all non-payment, fraud and end-customer chargeback loss. Reseller Sched. 4.5 says do not put a name into DNS until payment is "reasonably assured". Note 14.7 concerns chargebacks on Mosshatch's own top-ups to CNR; a chargeback by an end customer against Mosshatch at its card processor is not 14.7 but is a 3.6 loss.

### 12. Expired-domain "utilization" clause - CONFIRMED (quote), one sub-claim CORRECTED, mitigation found
- R&M 1.7 verbatim incl. "dispose, auction, transfer to third parties or take over the Domain Name in his own continuance", "no earlier than ... five (5) days after the expiration ...", "no renewal shall be possible 30 days after the expiration".
- CORRECTED: the dossier says "the MSA takes precedence [over RA's 14 days] under its clause 30". Clause 30 ranks the MSA, Schedules, Appendices, Anti-Abuse Policy, general T&C and Third-Party Policies. It does not list the Registration Agreement, which is a separate contract between Key-Systems and each registrant. The registrant will have accepted RA s.5 ("no earlier than 14 days after the expiration"), and RA s.5 / T&C 4 also say a customer who has not paid renewal "3 days before the expiry ... loses his rights to the domain". Three different timelines (3 days before, +5 days, +14 days) coexist; the KB's 35-day ARGP is a fourth. Do not promise registrants a specific grace period.
- Mitigation the dossier missed: R&M 1.7 opens "Unless otherwise agreed", and KB-SUBRES lists an account setting "Domain utilisation - Allow Key-Systems to take over domains flagged for expiration or deletion in their own portfolio as soon as the domains enter their respective deletion phase", inheritable and lockable. That is documented for Subreseller settings; I found no documentation of the same toggle for the top-level account, so ask CNR to confirm in writing that utilisation is off for Mosshatch's account.
- Flow-down duty (R&M 1.7): Mosshatch must "require the Registrant to agree as part of the Registration Agreement" that non-payment/non-renewal is consent to those actions, including transfer and change of registrant.

### 14. Provider may act directly on customers' domains and contact customers - CONFIRMED, and broader than stated

### 16. B2B attestation "binds the account holder, not its end customers" - CORRECTED / UNRESOLVED (possible blocker)
SIGN text verbatim: "CentralNic Reseller is a B2B service that does not provide services to private consumers or individuals based in the EU. I therefore hereby confirm that this account will only be used by either a legally established business entity or for the management of domain portfolios held outside the EU and not be used by private individuals based within the EU. I agree that this account may be terminated with immediate effect if CentralNic Reseller learns that I do not meet the above eligibility criteria unless CentralNic Reseller has previously granted explicit written permission for such use." The dossier asserts (s.1.1 table) that this "binds the account holder ..., not its end customers". The text does not say that; the headline sentence says the service is not provided to private individuals in the EU, and the "or for the management of domain portfolios held outside the EU" limb suggests the concern is the portfolio's holders. Reseller Sched. 2 ("No Consumer") is about Mosshatch itself. PROG says resellers "sell domains to the public", which points the other way. The documents conflict; if Mosshatch sells to EU natural persons, get explicit written permission before onboarding, because the stated remedy is immediate account termination.

### 18. "Does CNR auto-renew by itself? Default mode = automatic deletion of domains at the end of the registration period" - REFUTED
KB-RENEW 3.2: "The renewal mode of a domain will be defined by your general account settings unless changed explicitly. The default account setting is AutoRenew." KB-RENEW 6.1: "CentralNic Reseller reseller accounts are by default pre-configured for auto-renew." KB-DAY1 step 6: "AUTORENEW - domains renew automatically before they expire (the safe default for most resellers)." MSA R&M 1.4.4: "Provided that Customer has sufficient funds and/or credit ... all Domain Name registrations will be automatically renewed ('Autorenew') unless the respective domain or the default renewal mode has been set by the Customer or Registrant to either 'Autoexpire', 'Autodelete' ...". The sentence the dossier quotes ("automatic deletion of domains at the end of the registration period") is the KB's summary of the AutoExpire/AutoDelete account setting, not the default. Consequence: unless Mosshatch changes the account mode, CNR renews and debits the prepaid balance on its own (and out of funds = domain left to expire and removed, KB-RENEW 4.2). Under Reseller Sched. 3.6 Mosshatch owes that money even if the end customer never pays.

### 22. Data-handling terms - NOT IN DOSSIER (added)
