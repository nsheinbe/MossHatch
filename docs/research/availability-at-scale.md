# Type-ahead availability at scale, RDAP/DNS pre-filters, and front-running

Research date and access date for every source: **2026-09-29**. Analyst: research subagent (live-verified). Scope: Mosshatch Phase 0, search "pool" wanting availability for .com .ai .dev .io .app .studio on each keystroke. Registrar availability-API costs and limits are out of scope (see the provider dossiers `reg-*.md`).

## TL;DR

1. RDAP and DNS can only prove a name is TAKEN, never that it is available. RDAP 404 and DNS NXDOMAIN also occur for registry-reserved names (live: example.dev/.app/.studio) and DNS NXDOMAIN occurs for registered names on hold (live: qvz.com, RDAP 200 'client hold'). Only the registrar's EPP-backed check, with fee data, is authoritative (F28-F33).
2. Endpoints from the IANA bootstrap (published 2026-09-28): .com Verisign, .dev/.app Google Registry, .ai/.studio Identity Digital. .io has NO bootstrap entry; Identity Digital's host answers .io anyway, undocumented (F01-F03, F06, F07).
3. No registry publishes a numeric RDAP limit, and all three ban 'high volume, automated' querying (Google: 'experimental' pilot, no exception; Verisign and Identity Digital: except 'as reasonably necessary to register domain names'). Identity Digital answered 429 (Cloudflare 1015, Retry-After about 12,000 s) to 5 of 13 of our requests. Keep RDAP off the per-keystroke path (F10, F12-F16).
4. Measured through a shared proxy: RDAP TTFB 0.13-0.47 s, DoH 0.12-0.36 s typical and 4.6 s for a lame delegation (F08-F10, F32).
5. Recommended shape: debounce to settled input, one server call fanning out to six TLDs, cache 'taken' for hours and 'not proven taken' for at most 5 minutes (SOA negative TTLs are 300-3600 s), authoritative registrar check only on select/hatch and again at checkout, ASCII-only labels in Phase 0 (section 5).
6. Front running: SAC 022 (Oct 2007) and SAC 024 (Feb 2008; 120 claims, 0% provable) found no proven case. The one documented registrar practice is Network Solutions' 4-day hold on searched names (class action settled, judgment 2009-10-28). New-gTLD registry agreements now ban registry front-running (Spec 9 1(c)); the 2013 RAA and the .com agreement contain no such clause (F39-F49).
7. Commitments to make: never register, reserve or hold a name because it was searched; never sell or share search data; no durable storage of search text (cache <= 5 min); plain-language notice at the search box (SAC 024 rec. 6); name every party that sees queries; publish canary-test results (section 7).
8. Unverified: numeric RDAP limits, a live premium example, .io/.ai IDN policy, and any post-2010 incident (WebSearch budget was exhausted at 200/200, so discovery was by direct URL only).

## 2. What an RDAP 404 (or DNS NXDOMAIN) does and does not mean

RFC 7480 defines 404 as "an empty result set" and tells servers holding information they will not show to use another 4xx (F17). RDAP is a database of **registration objects** ("for all registered ... domain names", F20). Anything that is unavailable for a reason other than an existing registration has no object, so it returns 404. The Registry Agreement makes that explicit for reserved labels (F21) and lets registries reserve or block more at their discretion (F22, F23); premium is a price attribute of an unregistered name that RDAP never carries (F27).

| Situation | RDAP | DNS via DoH | Registrar availability | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Registered and resolving | 200 | NOERROR + answers | unavailable | example.com, google.dev, google.app live (F08, F09) |
| Registered, no A record | 200 | NOERROR, empty answer (NODATA) | unavailable | example.ai live (F32) |
| Registered, EPP `client hold` | 200, status lists "client hold" | **NXDOMAIN** | unavailable | qvz.com live, registered 1997 (F31) |
| Registered, lame delegation | 200 | **SERVFAIL** after 4.6 s (EDE 22) | unavailable | zxq.com live (F32) |
| Registry-reserved label (Spec 5 "example") | **404** | **NXDOMAIN** | unavailable | example.dev, example.app, example.studio live (F29) |
| Allocated to registry or ICANN | 200 | resolves or not | unavailable | nic.dev, nic.app, icann.dev, internic.app live (F29) |
| Unregistered, standard price | 404 | NXDOMAIN | **available** | random label on all six TLDs live (F29) |
| Unregistered, premium | 404 (expected) | NXDOMAIN | available at a higher fee only via fee data | not observed (F27, F28); unverified |
| Registry-discretion block or DPML block | 404 (expected) | NXDOMAIN | unavailable | not observed (F22, F23) |
| Pending delete / redemption | 200 with status | varies | unavailable, may drop soon | RFC 9083 status (F62); not observed |
| Throttled or failing pre-filter | 429 text/plain, 5xx, timeout | SERVFAIL, timeout | n/a | Identity Digital live (F10) |

Bottom line: **200/delegated proves "taken". 404/NXDOMAIN proves nothing about availability.** The new-gTLD agreement itself says NXDOMAIN covers "not registered", "no NS records" and "status does not allow them to be published" alike (F33). Only EPP `domain:check` `avail` (F28) plus fee data answers "can I buy it, and for how much".

## 3. DNS-over-HTTPS as a pre-filter

Tested against Cloudflare's JSON DoH (`cloudflare-dns.com/dns-query`): cached answers 0.12-0.36 s; in a 12-name spot check of three-letter .com names three took 3.2-4.6 s and three took 0.8-1.1 s because of slow or lame authorities (F32). Weaknesses, all demonstrated or contractually documented:

1. **One-sided signal.** Positive answers (NS/A present, or SERVFAIL from a lame delegation) imply a delegation exists; NXDOMAIN implies nothing (F31, F33). SAC 022 already called NXDOMAIN "a less accurate method than querying a registry or WHOIS" (F35).
2. **Caching skew.** Resolvers cache the negative answer for the SOA MINIMUM: .com 900 s, .dev 300 s, .app 900 s, .ai 3600 s, .studio 3600 s, .io 3600 s (F34). A name registered a minute ago can show NXDOMAIN for up to an hour.
3. **Privacy and front-running surface.** Every name goes to a third party: Cloudflare keeps resolver logs up to 25 hours (F36), Google 24-48 hours with IP and query (F37), and SAC 022 lists DNS operators collecting NXDOMAIN data as a front-running vector (F35).
4. **Limits.** Google Public DNS applies per-IP rate limits (F38); Cloudflare's JSON page states none. Neither is a contract for programmatic use.
5. **Tail latency** from lame delegations (4.6 s) requires hard timeouts.

Verdict: DoH adds a cheap "taken" signal but leaks searches to a third party, and everything it proves is also proven by RDAP 200, a local zone copy or the registrar check. Prefer a local zone-file copy where available (F61) or skip DNS.

## 4. Signal comparison (coverage matrix; no per-call fee for any of these)

| Signal | Proves taken? | Proves available? | Sees reserved/blocked/premium? | Cost / limits | Privacy exposure | Freshness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Local static rules (Spec 5 seeds, syntax) | for seeds: "not available" | no | reserved only | none | none | static |
| RDAP 200 (Verisign, Google, Identity Digital) | yes | no | no | terms ban high volume; IDD 429 live; Google "experimental" | registry sees label + Mosshatch egress IP; Google retains query details (F14) | near real time, "not authoritative" (F16) |
| DoH delegation | yes (delegated) | no | no | per-IP limits (F38) | third-party resolver logs 25 h-48 h | up to SOA MINIMUM stale (F34) |
| Zone-file copy (CZDS) | yes for new gTLDs | no | no | free; <=1 download/24 h; lawful use, no high-volume queries to registry (F61) | none at query time | up to 24 h stale |
| Registrar EPP check via reseller API | yes (reason text) | **yes** | yes with fee data (F28) | provider quotas (see provider dossiers) | registrar and registry see label (unavoidable) | real time |

**Verdict on RDAP as a cheap pre-filter.** Technically it is a usable, free, fast (about 0.15-0.5 s) "taken" confirmer, and Verisign and Google showed no throttling in 6 and 18 requests. But (1) it can never say "available", (2) all three operators' terms forbid high-volume automated use with no number to design against, (3) Google calls its service experimental and logs queries, (4) Identity Digital throttled 5 of 13 requests, (5) .io has no official endpoint, and (6) it adds three more parties that see every searched label. For Phase 0 keep it off the keystroke path and default off; if it is enabled, use it only for settled input with caching and a circuit breaker.

## 5. Design guidance (b)

Items marked (R) are my recommendations built on the cited facts, not sourced numbers.

**5.1 Pipeline shape (R).** Tier 0: client-side syntax and static "not available" rules, zero network. Tier 1: server cache of prior results. Tier 2 (optional, default off for Phase 0): "taken" confirmation from RDAP or a local zone copy for settled input only. Tier 3: registrar authoritative check with fee data when the user selects or hatches, and again before creating the Stripe Checkout session. UI states: taken (proven), likely available (not proven taken), verified available (with quote time), premium, not available, unknown.

**5.2 Debounce and fan-out (R).** Fire on ~300-400 ms idle (or Enter/blur), minimum 3 characters, abort superseded fetches, single Mosshatch endpoint returning all six TLD states, singleflight coalescing per label. Browsers must not call registries directly even though all three RDAP hosts send `access-control-allow-origin: *`, because that exposes user IPs to registries and bypasses caching. Load illustration: 3 settled queries x 6 TLDs = 18 upstream lookups per session before caching; 1,000 sessions per hour is 5 lookups per second sustained, against per-IP throttles and terms that give no number.

**5.3 Cache TTLs (R with the facts they rest on).**

| Result | TTL | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| IANA bootstrap | per HTTP `Expires` (IANA serves max-age 86400) | RFC 9224: "Clients SHOULD NOT fetch the registry on every RDAP request"; live max-age=86400 (F59, F01) |
| RDAP 200 / delegated ("taken") | 1-24 h | Expired names pass a 45-day auto-renew grace and a 30-day redemption grace before release (Google's .dev policy; other registries may differ); exception: a name deleted inside the 5-day Add Grace Period reopens (F63); `pending delete` status means it may drop soon (F62) |
| RDAP 404 / NXDOMAIN ("not proven taken") | 60-300 s | Shortest SOA negative TTL is 300 s (.dev), longest 3600 s (F34); state flips whenever anyone registers |
| Registrar "available" | <= 60 s for display; never trusted at checkout | RFC 5731: avail holds "at the moment the check command was completed" (F28) |
| Registrar "unavailable" | 15 min-6 h | reserved names are stable; taken names change slowly |
| 429 / 5xx / timeout | not cached as an answer; back off per Retry-After; circuit breaker per upstream | RFC 7480 5.5 (F18); IDD Retry-After about 12,000 s (F10) |

**5.4 Authoritative check only on select/hatch (R).** Selecting a creature triggers the registrar check with fee data and shows a quote timestamp. Hatching re-checks immediately before Stripe Checkout creation and handles the race where the name is taken between check and create (cancel and refund). Registrar calls have their own quotas (provider dossiers), so the select-only rule also protects them.

**5.5 Premium and reserved names.** Premium status and price exist only in the registrar channel (F27, F28); the Vercel availability endpoint returns just a boolean (F30). Reserved sources to seed a local "not available" set: Spec 5 labels EXAMPLE, NIC, WWW, RDDS, WHOIS, two-character labels (release authorisations introduced 2016-12-13, F24; already registerable on .dev, F25), country and territory names, IOC/Red Cross and IGO names (F21); the .com schedule adds aso, gnso, icann, internic, ccnso, afrinic, apnic, arin, example, gtld-servers, iab, iana, iana-servers, iesg, ietf, irtf, istf, lacnic, latnic, rfc-editor, ripe, root-servers and all single-character labels (F26). Many of these appear as registered in RDAP (nic.dev, icann.dev, internic.app), so the label should read "not available", never "reserved" as a claim, and never be used to infer availability of anything else.

**5.6 IDN and homographs.** Phase 0: ASCII LDH only. Rules: lowercase; 1-63 octets (RFC 1035, F56); no leading or trailing hyphen and no "--" in positions 3-4 unless it is a valid A-label (RFC 5891, F56). Later, with IDN: normalise with UTS #46 to A-labels before any RDAP/DNS/registrar call (RDAP accepts all-A or all-U labels, F55); require a single script or UTS #39 "Highly Restrictive" (F58, v18.0.0 rev 34 dated 2026-08-27); apply ICANN IDN Guidelines v4.0 items 15-17 (single script per label, whole-script confusables, F54); intersect with each registry's IANA IDN table (IANA lists 97 .com, 25 .dev, 25 .app and 16 .studio table files and none for .io or .ai, F57); show the punycode next to the Unicode form; block lookalikes of a maintained brand list; treat mixed-script as a hard reject.

**5.7 Blocklists.** Beyond registry reserved lists, Mosshatch needs its own abuse/brand blocklist; no primary source defines one, so it is a product decision with legal input (section 10).

**5.8 Agents.** Scoped "search" tokens get their own quota and logging; they can enumerate at machine speed and would otherwise push Mosshatch over upstream terms. Agents are bound by the same no-registration-on-search invariant.

## 6. Front-running: what has been documented and what ICANN says (c)

**6.1 Definition and stance.** SSAC (ICANN's Security and Stability Advisory Committee, an advisory body) coined "domain name front running" in SAC 022 (October 2007): checking availability "can be a sensitive act which may disclose an interest in or a value ascribed to a domain name" (F39). It listed who can observe checks: client software, third-party WHOIS portals, DNS operators (NXDOMAIN data), **registrars and resellers**, **name spinners** (cross-TLD fan-out, which is Mosshatch's design) and registries (F40), and noted the RAA and Registry Agreements did not prohibit monitoring or selling the data (F41).

**6.2 Documented cases and studies.**

| Date | Event | Outcome | Findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2007-10 | SAC 022 call for reports; two alleged incidents from an IP attorney, undisclosed | no hard data | F39-F41 |
| 2007-12-14 to 2008-06-26 | **Network Solutions "Customer Protection Measure"**: searched names registered in NSI's own name for up to four days (test 2007-12-14; launch 2008-01-07 per the settlement, 2008-01-08 per SAC 024; opt-in from 2008-03-15; stopped 2008-06-26) | Presented by NSI as protection against front running; litigated as an undisclosed practice | F44 F45 |
| 2008-02-25 | McElroy v. Network Solutions, C.D. Cal. 2:08-cv-01247 (plus Finseth) | class of 113,094 notified, 1 objection, 16 opt-outs; final judgment 2009-10-28; $6 credit or $9.91 per registration; fees $171,994; NSI denied wrongdoing | F45 |
| 2008-02 | SAC 024 analysed 120 claims | 0% provable; 37% tasting, 25% competition, 19% unstudyable, 10% non-renewal, 8% typo-squats; the studies "do not disprove the possibility that domain front running occurs" | F42 |
| 2008-12-17 | AGP Limits Policy | curbs volume tasting (10% or 50 names per month) | F50 |
| 2009-06-16 | Edelman test for ICANN | 600+ searches, 7 days of checks, none registered; cannot prove absence | F47 |
| 2010-05-29 | GNSO RAPWG final report | one confirmed case (NSI, via SAC 024); members "generally considered front-running an abuse"; **no policy development recommended**, monitor | F46 |
| 2014-2015 (agreement versions read) | New-gTLD Registry Agreement Spec 9 1(c) | registries barred from registering names based on proprietary access to searches or resolution requests; earlier versions unverified | F48 |
| after 2010 | none found in the sources I could open | search budget exhausted; unverified | section 10 |

**6.3 What ICANN says now (contract level).** Registries of new gTLDs (.dev, .app, .studio) may not front-run (F48). The 2013 RAA and the 2024 .com Registry Agreement contain no front-running clause (F49); .ai and .io sit outside ICANN's registry contract. Registrars and resellers face no ICANN front-running rule I could find; their duties come from their own registrar or reseller agreement and general consumer law (legal review, section 10); SSAC's recommendations for them are advisory: give clear notice of how availability-check data is treated (F43). SSAC also says an agent that collects a user's interest and discloses it publicly "violates a trust relationship" (F43).

**6.4 Lesson from the Network Solutions case.** A registrar "protective" hold placed on search, even framed as anti-front-running, was litigated as an undisclosed, coercive practice (F45). Mosshatch should not add any search-triggered hold, watch or back-order without explicit opt-in.

**6.5 Registry-side collection of search terms exists.** Identity Digital's privacy policy describes a "Domain Engine" product that uses the search terms entered on a registrar's website and the visitor IP (F53). Ask the upstream registrar whether any such widget touches search input.

## 7. Public commitments Mosshatch should make (draft wording, for legal review)

1. **No registration on search.** "Mosshatch will never register, reserve, hold or back-order a domain name because someone searched for it. A name is registered only after you approve the purchase with your passkey and pay." Extend it to staff, contractors and AI agents; forbid personal registration of names seen in search data.
2. **No sale or sharing.** "We do not sell, license or share what you search for. No advertising or analytics partner sees your searches."
3. **Say who sees a search.** "To check a name we send it to our registrar partner [name] and, through it, to the registry. [If RDAP is used: for .com, .dev, .app, .ai and .studio we may also query Verisign, Google Registry and Identity Digital.]" (SAC 022 vectors, F40; SAC 024 rec. 6, F43.)
4. **Log retention.** Search text is not written to durable storage; an in-memory cache keyed by label lives at most 5 minutes; abuse counters keyed by hashed token/IP live at most 24 hours; only aggregate counts remain (R, benchmarked on 25 h and 24-48 h resolver retention, F36, F37). Purchase and registration records are kept as the registrar's ICANN duties require: registration data for the sponsorship period plus 2 years, transaction logs at least 180 days (F51, F52); state this separately so it is not mistaken for search retention.
5. **Not a guarantee about others.** Registries and registrars we depend on also see checks. Publish which of them contractually forbid front running (new-gTLD registries, F48) and which do not (.com: no clause found in the agreement text, F49; .ai and .io: outside ICANN's Registry Agreement, their own terms unverified).
6. **Proof.** Run and publish a recurring canary test (Edelman method, F47) and give users a front-running report form asking for the SSAC-listed details (method, times, responses, correspondence, F39).
7. **Agents.** The same promises bind agent tokens; agent search calls are quota-limited and logged under the same retention.

## 10. Unverified items and items for a lawyer or accountant

**Unverified (and why)**

- Numeric RDAP rate limits for Verisign, Google Registry and Identity Digital: none published on the terms or help pages read; not measured, because burst testing would breach the 'polite requests' rule and the registry terms.
- Why Identity Digital returned 429 to 5 of 13 requests (the last two succeeded): the egress is a shared proxy IP, so it may reflect other tenants; the Retry-After of about 12,000 s suggests a pre-existing block, not our 13 requests. Attribution unverified.
- Whether Identity Digital officially supports RDAP for .io (it answers today, but .io has no IANA RDAP entry) and whether that will persist.
- A live premium-name example: 50 common words across 5 TLDs were all registered or unavailable, and the Vercel availability endpoint returns only a boolean, so premium behaviour is documented (Google pricing policy, RFC 8748) but not observed.
- RDAP behaviour for DPML-blocked labels, registry-discretion blocks, pending-delete and redemption names: expected from ICANN text and RFC 9083, not observed live.
- IDN policy of the .ai and .io registries (IANA IDN repository has zero tables for either); IDN acceptance by Mosshatch's chosen registrar.
- Whether Vercel Functions can open raw DNS/WHOIS sockets (UDP/TCP 53, TCP 43) and what their default egress IP pool looks like; the Static IPs page does not say.
- .com zone-file access (Verisign programme) terms and whether CZDS grants are practical for a small reseller; .ai/.io zone files.
- Any front-running incident, complaint or registrar commitment after 2010: WebSearch was exhausted (200/200) and Bing/DuckDuckGo scraping returned nothing usable; only direct primary URLs (ICANN, court docket, registries) were read. Registrar pages scanned for 'front running' (Porkbun FAQ, Cloudflare Registrar, Dynadot, Hover) had no hits; Namecheap, GoDaddy pages returned 403.
- Whether the upstream registrar's reseller agreement flows down RAA data-retention and availability-check notice duties (RAA 3.12 allows it); depends on the provider chosen.
- Wikipedia-only claims not adopted (secondary source): that Tucows was accused of front running, and that Network Solutions registered every searched domain; the court settlement's own wording (registered in NSI's own name for a limited time, up to four days) is used instead.
- Current (post-2015) text of the .dev, .app and .studio Registry Agreements after ICANN's later global amendments; the 2014/2015 versions linked from ICANN were read.

**Needs a lawyer or accountant**

- Privacy notice and legal basis for search strings plus IP/session (GDPR/UK GDPR, CCPA) and the retention periods proposed in section 7.
- Whether per-keystroke or even per-settled-query RDAP use breaches the registries' terms (Google: experimental, no exception; Verisign and Identity Digital: 'reasonably necessary to register domain names') or needs written permission.
- Wording of public 'no front-running / no selling of search data' commitments: they must stay true when third parties (registrar, registries, DoH resolvers) also see queries, otherwise they risk being deceptive claims.
- Any pre-purchase hold, watch or back-order feature: the Network Solutions precedent (McElroy, C.D. Cal. 2:08-cv-01247) shows a 'protective' hold placed on search drew a certified class action; require explicit opt-in terms and legal review.
- Flow-down terms with the upstream registrar under RAA 3.12: data retention (registration data plus 2 years, transaction logs 180 days), availability-check data handling, and registry-data use restrictions.
- Homograph and trademark exposure if IDN registration is enabled later (UDRP/ACPA, brand-lookalike blocking policy).
