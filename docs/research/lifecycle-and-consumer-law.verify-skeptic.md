# Skeptic verification: lifecycle-and-consumer-law (Mosshatch Phase 0)

Verifier date: 2026-09-29. Lens: ERRP notice schedule, AGP limits thresholds, state of the FTC click-to-cancel rule, California ARL 2025 changes, EU withdrawal-right treatment of domains.
Method: every verdict below was re-derived from a page or PDF I fetched myself this session (curl through the proxy with a browser UA; nothing taken from the analyst's raw files). Working copies are in vs-lcl/ next to this file. WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200), so discovery was by navigating primary index pages and APIs. Blocked and not worked around: nysenate.gov (Cloudflare challenge), DuckDuckGo (bot challenge), Cellar default (needed an Accept header, which is ordinary content negotiation, not a bypass).

## Headline

The analyst's lens claims hold up. Nothing was refuted. Four items need correction or refinement:
1. ERRP "mandatory since 21 Aug 2025" is misleading: the substantive ERRP (notice schedule, DNS interruption, RGP, fee display) has bound registrars since 31 Aug 2013. The 21 Feb 2024 revision only swapped terminology (Registered Name Holder, RDDS, Registration Data Reminder Policy); I diffed the 2013 and 2024 texts and no timing or duty changed.
2. The New York effective date is now resolved (analyst had it unverified): S.3008-C/A.3008-C was signed as Chapter 58 of the Laws of 2025 on 9 May 2025; Part W says it takes effect on the 180th day after enactment, which computes to 5 Nov 2025. It is in force now.
3. "No NPRM" is now backed by the FTC's own Unified Agenda entry (RIN 3084-AB84, prerule stage), which the analyst could not read. The last timetable item is "Staff Review of Public Comments 07/00/2026"; there is no NPRM date.
4. On EU domains, the analyst's practical inference ("an EU consumer can probably withdraw within 14 days") is unsupported either way; the one registrar contract I read (Gandi, French law) takes the opposite view (service "fully executed" at registration/renewal, consumer waiver).

## Claim-by-claim

### A. ERRP (ICANN)
Sources: https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/consensus-policies/expired-registration-recovery-policy/expired-registration-recovery-policy-21-02-2024-en (fetched, 14.9k chars); 2013 version at .../expired-registration-recovery-policy-28-02-2013-en; index https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/consensus-policies.

- Two pre-expiry notices, "approximately one month" and "approximately one week"; safe harbour 26-35 and 4-10 days. CONFIRMED. 2.1.1 text plus the Notes paragraph "transmitted between 26-35 days and between 4-10 days prior to expiration, respectively, this would be considered compliant". The duty reads "Prior to the expiration of any gTLD registration, registrars must notify ... at least two times", with no auto-renew carve-out in the text.
- Additional notice within five days after expiry. CONFIRMED with a reading caveat. 2.1.2: "If a registration is not renewed by the RAE or deleted by the registrar, within five days after the expiration of the registration, the registrar must transmit at least one additional expiration notice ... that includes instructions for renewing". The comma placement allows two readings: (a) the notice is due within 5 days of expiry (the analyst's reading); (b) it is owed only if the name is still unrenewed/undeleted at day 5 (timing after that unspecified). Sending at E+1 satisfies both.
- Notice language and channel (language of the registration agreement; email, no affirmative action to receive). CONFIRMED (2.1.3).
- Registrars may delete any time after expiry; no minimum grace; no price rule. CONFIRMED (2.2.1, and 4.1 asks only for "post-expiration renewal fees (if different)").
- DNS: interrupted from expiry to deletion if deleted within 8 days; otherwise at least the last 8 consecutive days of the renewable period, "to the extent the applicable registry permits". Example 1 Oct expiry / 20 Oct deletion => 12-20 Oct. CONFIRMED (2.2.2, 2.2.3, Notes).
- Redirect page must conspicuously say expired and give renewal instructions; renewal permitted through the interruption period; DNS restored immediately or as soon as commercially reasonable. CONFIRMED (2.2.4-2.2.6). Notes add the page duty applies whenever the name is still renewable.
- 30-day RGP; DNS disabled and transfers prohibited; registrar must allow redemption. CONFIRMED (3.1-3.3).
- Fees at registration (renewal, post-expiry renewal if different, restore) on the registrar site, linked from the agreement, and on resellers' sites; notification methods on resellers' sites. CONFIRMED (4.1, 4.1.1, 4.1.2, 4.2.3). "Encouraged" prominence language CONFIRMED (Notes).
- Currency date. CORRECTED. Index page: ERRP is a listed consensus policy, marked "*" = updated 21 Feb 2024, "must implement no later than 21 August 2025". The current policy's own Notes still say "All registrars and registries are required to comply with this policy beginning 31 August 2013". Diff of the 2013 vs 2024 text: only terminology changes. Corrected value: substantive ERRP duties in force since 31 Aug 2013; only the terminology update had the 21 Aug 2025 deadline.
- ICANN lifecycle chart 5 / 0-45 / 30 / 5. CONFIRMED by viewing https://www.icann.org/en/registrars/gtld-lifecycle.jpg (5-day Add-Grace, Registered 1-10 y, Auto-Renew Grace 0-45 d, Redemption Grace 30 d "aka Pending Delete - Restorable", Pending Delete 5 d, Released; JPEG metadata dated 2007). EPP status page https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/epp-status-codes-2014-06-16-en: redemptionPeriod "held ... 30 days. After five calendar days following the end of the redemptionPeriod ... purged"; addPeriod "registry may provide credit"; autoRenewPeriod "registry provides a credit ... for the cost of the renewal".

### C. AGP Limits Policy
Source: https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/consensus-policies/add-grace-period-limits-policy/agp-add-grace-period-limits-policy-17-12-2008-en (also reachable via the index link /resources/pages/agp-policy-2008-12-17-en).
- Formula: an Operator shall not refund AGP deletes exceeding "(i) 10% of that Registrar's net new registrations ... in that month, or (ii) fifty (50) domain names, whichever is greater". CONFIRMED; per TLD per month; net new registrations counts one-year through ten-year registrations as one each.
- Worked thresholds 100 -> 50, 500 -> 50, 1,000 -> 100, 5,000 -> 500. CONFIRMED arithmetically from the formula; ICANN's own example (1,000 net adds, 250 AGP deletes -> refund for 100, 150 excess) CONFIRMED verbatim.
- Refinement (missed by analyst): "net adds" in the registry reporting spec excludes names deleted in the AGP. .dev registry agreement Specification 3: "net-adds-1-yr ... number of domains successfully registered ... with an initial term of one (1) year (and not deleted within the add grace period). A transaction must be reported in the month the add grace period ends." Source https://itp.cdn.icann.org/en/files/registry-agreements/dev/dev-agmt-html-16oct14-en.htm. So an AGP-heavy registrar's cap is computed on adds after removing its own AGP deletes; Mosshatch cannot compute the pool itself from its own gross numbers.
- AGP "typically the five-day period", "appears as a contractual term in some, but not all gTLD registry agreements". CONFIRMED. Exemptions need extraordinary circumstances not known, not knowable and outside the registrar's control; recurring ones do not count; purpose is anti-tasting. CONFIRMED. Policy effective 1 Apr 2009 CONFIRMED on the index page.
- The pool belongs to the sponsoring registrar per TLD, not to a reseller: CONFIRMED by the policy text ("every Registrar is entitled to the threshold number ... for each TLD"). Name.com's isRefundable "already accounts for ICANN AGP limits, reseller-level thresholds, and name.com refund policies" CONFIRMED at https://docs.name.com/guides/refunds-flow.

Reporter cite 142 F.4th 1060 CONFIRMED in the FTC's own footnotes (91 FR 6507 n.3; ANPRM n.40, pin 1070-75). Amended rule had effective date 14 Jan 2025 and compliance extended from 14 May to 14 Jul 2025 (ANPRM; opinion), so its main duties never became binding.
- Old rule restored 12 Feb 2026. CONFIRMED: 91 FR 6507, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-12/pdf/2026-02866.pdf ("revises the Negative Option Rule to restore it in the form it existed before the 2024 Rule became effective"; "effective February 12, 2026"). eCFR (title 16 up to date as of 2026-09-25) shows Part 425 "USE OF PRENOTIFICATION NEGATIVE OPTION PLANS", "Source: 91 FR 6509, Feb. 12, 2026", 425.1 The rule, 425.2 [Reserved].
- Restored rule covers only prenotification plans. CONFIRMED (ANPRM: "Prenotification plans are the only negative option practice currently covered by the Commission's Negative Option Rule"; automatic renewals are listed as a separate, uncovered category).
- ANPRM 13 Mar 2026, comments closed 13 Apr 2026. CONFIRMED: 91 FR 12318, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-03-13/pdf/2026-04952.pdf, "Comments must be received on or before April 13, 2026", RIN 3084-AB54. - No NPRM as of 29 Sep 2026. The FTC's FR-published Regulatory Agenda of 14 Aug 2026 (91 FR 53156) lists "the removal of the vacated 2024 amendments to the Negative Option Rule" among current rulemakings and nothing further. Caveat: absence of a scheduled NPRM is an agenda snapshot, not a promise; I did not check the 8th Cir. docket for any rehearing petition, but the FTC's Feb 2026 rule and its agenda treat the vacatur as final.

### E. California ARL (AB 2863)
Fetched leginfo BPC 17600-17606.
- Chapter and effective date: "Amended by Stats. 2024, Ch. 515 (AB 2863) Effective January 1, 2025" on 17601 and 17602. CONFIRMED. 17601(b) and 17602(j): amendments "shall only apply to a contract entered into, amended, or extended under this article on or after July 1, 2025". CONFIRMED (so each domain auto-renewal after that date is an "extended" contract on the statute's own wording).
- 17602(b)(2): initial term of one year or longer that auto-renews: notice "at least 15 days and not more than 45 days before the automatic renewal offer or continuous service offer renews". CONFIRMED.
- 17602(g)(2): fee change notice "no less than 7 days and no more than 30 days before the fee change takes effect" with cancel information. CONFIRMED. 17602(h) annual reminder with product, frequency and amount, means to cancel. CONFIRMED. 17602(a)(6) consent verification "at least three years, or one year after the contract is terminated, whichever period is longer". CONFIRMED.
- 17602(a)(1) visual proximity; (a)(2) affirmative consent before charging; (a)(4) express affirmative consent; (a)(3) retainable acknowledgment; (d)(1) online sign-up => exclusively online, at will termination; (e)(2) retention offer only with a simultaneous "click to cancel" link. CONFIRMED.
- Additions the analyst did not state: 17601(a)(4) defines "consumer" as an individual buying "for personal, family, or household purposes", so CA ARL does not reach business purchasers (this answers, for CA only, the analyst's B2B open question). 17602(d)(3) lets a business require account authentication before online termination, so requiring nothing extra to switch OFF is stricter than CA needs. 17604(a): a violation is not a crime but civil remedies apply; 17604(b): good-faith compliance shields from civil remedies. 17603 unconditional-gift rule is limited to goods.

### G. EU withdrawal right and domains
Sources: consolidated CRD https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/02011L0083-20220528 (needs Accept: application/xhtml+xml); Commission guidance https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/52021XC1229%2804%29; Directive 2023/2673 https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32023L2673.
- Art. 9(1) 14 days, no reason; 9(2)(a) service contracts run from conclusion. CONFIRMED.
- Art. 16(a): exception only "after the service has been fully performed" and, if payment is owed, only if performance began "with the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgement that he will lose his right of withdrawal once the contract has been fully performed". CONFIRMED.
- Art. 14(3) proportionate amount; guidance cites C-641/19 PE Digital pro rata temporis. CONFIRMED. Art. 22 default options. CONFIRMED. Art. 6(1) termination conditions for automatically extended contracts (paragraph on duration "or is to be extended automatically"). CONFIRMED.
- Guidance: express consent is "a positive action ... such as ticking a box"; "pre-ticked box or ... a clause in the general terms and conditions ... would not satisfy". CONFIRMED.
- "No domain-specific exclusion" and "guidance never mentions domain names": CONFIRMED for both texts (zero occurrences of "domain name" in the consolidated CRD and in the 2021 guidance). "No guidance/authority anywhere": UNVERIFIABLE (could not search national case law, DFA, or regulator FAQs; WebSearch exhausted).
- Analyst's practical inference that an EU consumer "can probably withdraw within 14 days and owes at most a proportionate amount": UNVERIFIABLE, flagged as inference by the analyst and I found no primary source either way. Counter-evidence from market practice: Gandi Domain name registration Contract v2024.1 (https://contract.gandi.net/v5/contracts/61283/DomainNameConditions_SAS_2024.1_en.pdf) Art. 7: "the Service provided by Gandi is fully executed for each domain name upon its registration, renewal or effective transfer ... since the Service is fully performed before the expiry of the legal withdrawal period ... You expressly waive Your right of withdrawal" (citing French Consumer Code L.221-28). That clause is a T&C waiver, which the Commission guidance says would not satisfy the express-consent requirement on its own, so the industry position is contestable too. Net: open legal question, lawyer item stands.
- Art. 11a "withdraw from contract here": Directive 2023/2673 inserts it; text "For distance contracts concluded by the means of an online interface, the trader shall ensure that the consumer can also withdraw ... by using a withdrawal function"; Member States "adopt and publish by 19 December 2025 ... shall apply those measures from 19 June 2026". CONFIRMED. It applies to distance contracts generally (financial-services contracts have a separate list), and matters only where a withdrawal right exists.
- UK: CCR 2013 reg. 36(2) (fully performed after request and acknowledgement), reg. 36 proportionate payment, reg. 40 no pre-ticked box; legislation.gov.uk states up to date to 29 Sep 2026. DMCCA 2024 s.254 status "Prospective" ("not in force at Royal Assent", up to date to 29 Sep 2026). Gov.uk news of 2 Apr 2026: "measures are expected to come into force in spring 2027"; reminders before 12-month+ renewals; "14 day period after a trial or 12 month+ contract auto-renews to cancel and receive a full or proportionate refund". CONFIRMED.

## Things the analyst missed or should tighten
1. Zero-margin timings in the recommended cadence. The dossier's own rule is a 2-day margin inside each window, but E-17 (= C-7) sits exactly on Visa's "at least 7 days" floor, and E-45 is exactly 45 days before E, so if a regulator measures California's "before the ... offer renews" from the term boundary E rather than the charge C, it sits on the 45-day edge. Suggest C-8 and E-43 or E-44 (still 33-34 days before C for VT/NY/VA).
2. ERRP and auto-renewed names: with C = E-10 a successful auto-renewal moves expiry before the one-week window, so no E-7 notice goes out for those names. The ERRP text is unconditional ("any gTLD registration"), and I found no ICANN guidance either way. The E+1 alternative reading (2.1.2 caveat above) is also worth a lawyer line.
3. ERRP dates framing (see correction above): do not tell upstreams or counsel the policy became binding in 2025.
4. AGP "net adds" excludes AGP-deleted names (Spec 3), so the pool cannot be estimated from gross sales.
5. NY effective date 5 Nov 2025 and NY measures its notice from the cancellation deadline (C or earlier), not the renewal date.
6. CA/VT/NY/VA "consumer" definitions limit these laws to individuals (CA confirmed above); ROSCA has no such definition in 8403. B2B carve-out matters less than the analyst feared for CA only.
7. Two different FTC RINs exist: 3084-AB54 on the ANPRM and 3084-AB84 on the Unified Agenda entry; search by either.
8. .io delegation risk (UK/Mauritius/Chagos process) and .io registry terms currency are outside this dossier and unassessed; worth a one-line risk flag if .io is sold.
9. Digital Fairness Act status still unchecked (Commission pages are script-rendered; my fetch returned nothing usable).
