# Registrar accreditation obligations and registrant-data rules for Mosshatch (reseller model)

Research date and access date for every source below: **2026-09-29**. Prepared for the Mosshatch Phase 0 plan. Research only; no code or repo files changed.

## TL;DR

- Mosshatch is a 'Reseller' under RAA 1.26; the upstream registrar answers to ICANN for it and must bind it in writing (RAA 3.12): no ICANN-accreditation claims, RAA-compliant registration agreement that names the registrar, ICANN education + Registrants' Benefits links, P/P Spec. [F02-F05]
- Registration Data Policy: effective 21 Aug 2025 (text revised 12 May 2026). Collect only registrant name/address/phone/email (+optional org, tech); no admin/billing; personal data redacted in RDAP where law requires; TDRP data kept 15 months, other RAA retention (sponsorship+2y, 180-day logs) stays. [F12-F19]
- WHOIS was sunset 28 Jan 2025 (RDAP only; .com/.name/.post caveat); the registrar publishes RDAP with an abuse contact, a reseller entity is optional; Mosshatch should not run WHOIS/RDAP. [F20-F23]
- Accuracy: verify registrant email or phone within 15 days of registration/transfer-in/change of registrant or verify manually / suspend; 7-day update and 15-day response duties; annual data reminder; ERRP renewal notices and fee disclosure explicitly reach reseller sites. [F09-F11, F30-F31]
- DNS Abuse amendments (5 Apr 2024): registrar must publish an abuse contact and promptly mitigate actionable DNS Abuse; ICANN says the registrar stays liable when it delegates to a reseller, so expect intake, forwarding, action and 2-year record duties in the reseller contract. [F24-F26]
- UDRP: lock and reply within 2 business days, implement after 10 business days; URS applies to .dev/.app/.studio (not .com). OFAC: the RAA does not impose it but a US Mosshatch must comply; Iran rule excludes domain registration for blocked persons; keep list screening. [F32-F40, F53]
- Privacy law: GDPR Art 3(2)/27 (EU rep), Art 28 vendor DPAs, SCC/DPF (appeal C-703/25 P pending), UK Addendum, CCPA only above $26.625m / 100k; NIS2 Art 6(22) expressly names resellers and applies regardless of size (72-hour data-access replies) - the least obvious finding. [F41-F48]
- ccTLDs: .ai (Government of Anguilla, run by Identity Digital) and .io (Internet Computer Bureau) sit outside the RAA, Registration Data Policy and URS; .ai follows UDRP, supports RDAP and allows privacy/proxy; .io has its own WIPO-run dispute policy and no IANA-listed RDAP. [F49-F52]
- Unverified: the chosen upstream's actual reseller agreement, the reformed Transfer Policy, the DPF appeal outcome, .ai/.io registry-registrar terms, NIS2 national transposition, and Mosshatch's own jurisdiction/revenue. Counsel is needed for role allocation (controller/processor), consent wording, erasure-vs-retention and OFAC scope.

## 2. What a reseller agreement must contain (consolidated)

RAA 3.12 says the registrar must have a written agreement with every reseller that "enable[s] Registrar to comply with and perform all of its obligations under this Agreement" [F02] and lists seven items (3.12.1-3.12.7) [F04, F05]. Other requirements reach resellers through Consensus Policies (ERRP) or because the registrar cannot meet its own duty without reseller action (Accuracy Spec, DNS Abuse, retention, UDRP lock). ICANN itself cites RAA 3.12 when it says a registrar remains liable for delegated abuse handling [F26]. Basis of each clause:

| # | Basis | Clause the reseller agreement needs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Express in RAA 3.12 | No use of ICANN / ICANN-Accredited Registrar logos or claims of ICANN accreditation without ICANN's written permission (3.12.1). |
| 2 | Express in RAA 3.12 | Registration agreement includes all RAA-required provisions and notices and any ICANN Consensus Policy notices, and identifies the sponsoring registrar or provides a means such as lookup.icann.org (3.12.2); reseller names the registrar on inquiry (3.12.3). |
| 3 | Express in RAA 3.12 | Compliance with the Privacy/Proxy Specification (3.12.4) and any future P/P accreditation program (which may require the registrar to prohibit resellers from knowingly accepting registrations from non-accredited P/P providers). |
| 4 | Express in RAA 3.12 | Customers receive a link to ICANN's registrant educational webpage (3.12.5, 3.16). |
| 5 | Express in RAA 3.12 | Reseller publishes or links the Registrants' Benefits and Responsibilities and takes no action inconsistent with it or with applicable law (3.12.7). |
| 6 | Express in RAA 3.12 | Registrar right and duty to enforce and cure when the reseller causes a breach (3.12.6, closing paragraph). |
| 7 | Express in ERRP | Renewal/redemption fees and notification methods displayed on the reseller's website (ERRP 4.1.2, 4.2.3); expiry notices sent as ERRP s2 requires. |
| 8 | Express via RAA 3.12.2 and 3.7.7 | The registrant-facing agreement contains 3.7.7.1-3.7.7.12 (accurate data, 7-day update, 15-day response, licensee liability, notices, jurisdiction, indemnity). |
| 9 | Enabling (3.12 lead-in: 'enable Registrar to comply with ... all of its obligations') | Verification hooks and timers for the RDDS Accuracy Program Specification (15 days, manual verification or suspension, re-verification on bounce, willful-inaccuracy suspension). |
| 10 | Enabling | Annual Registration Data Reminder delivery and proof; data collection limited to Registration Data Policy s6 fields; registrant-organization / tech-contact notices. |
| 11 | Enabling (ICANN advisory footnote 11 cites 3.12) | Abuse intake, forwarding, action on registrar instruction (clientHold/lock), receipt confirmation, and 2-year records that the registrar can hand to ICANN. |
| 12 | Enabling | UDRP/URS lock and decision-implementation timelines (2 business days lock; 10 business days to implement), transfer lock and change-of-registrant handling. |
| 13 | Enabling | Retention and delivery of registration data and correspondence (term + 2 years; TDRP data 15 months; logs 180 days) and cooperation with ICANN audits (3.4, 3.15). |
| 14 | Registry-driven | Insertion of registry-mandated registrant terms (new gTLD Spec 11 s3(a); Verisign RRA 2.7(b)) and auth-code SLAs. |
| 15 | Law-driven (not in RAA) | Data-protection terms (controller/processor roles, sub-processors, transfers, breach notice, deletion) and sanctions/screening clauses. |

## 6. Topic-by-topic summary

### 6.1 RAA section 3.12 and reseller flow-down [F02-F06, F55]
The 2013 RAA (current text dated 21 Jan 2024, amended by the 5 Apr 2024 Global Amendment) makes the registrar fully responsible for reseller conduct and requires a written agreement. The seven express items are logo/accreditation claims, registration-agreement content and identification of the registrar, naming the registrar on inquiry, Privacy/Proxy Specification compliance, the ICANN educational link, enforcement when a reseller causes a breach, and publication of the Registrants' Benefits and Responsibilities. A diff of section 3.12 between the archived 2013 text and today's shows one change only: the lookup example changed from InterNIC Whois to lookup.icann.org (F06). A reseller is defined broadly (RAA 1.26) and must be listed to ICANN on request (Registrar Information Specification items 26-27).

### 6.2 Registration Data Policy and redaction [F12-F19]
Effective 21 Aug 2025, replacing the Interim policy; revised 12 May 2026 to add urgent-request timelines that only take effect when an ICANN authentication mechanism exists. It sets the collected data set (no admin/billing contact), the registrar-to-registry transfer conditions (legal basis + DPA), redaction rules (must redact where law requires, may redact anywhere), a web form in place of the registrant email, consent-to-publish, disclosure-request handling (2 business days to acknowledge, 30 calendar days to respond) and 15-month TDRP retention. The registrar decides whether to redact globally; Mosshatch should prefer an upstream that does.

### 6.3 RDAP replacing WHOIS [F20-F23]
WHOIS obligations sunset on 28 Jan 2025 (Ramp-Up period end 3 Feb 2024 + 360 days per RDDS Specification 1.1.5-1.1.6); RDAP under the Feb 2024 gTLD RDAP Profile has been mandatory since 21 Aug 2025. The RDAP response must carry registrar identity and IANA ID and an abuse entity with phone and email; a reseller entity is optional; redactions must be flagged, and redacted email can be replaced by a contact URI. ICANN's RDAP page still excepts .com, .name and .post from WHOIS relief; how that applies to registrars is unverified.

### 6.4 Accuracy, verification and reminders [F09-F11]
The RDDS Accuracy Program Specification (formerly WHOIS Accuracy) requires validation and verification within 15 days of registration, inbound transfer or change of registrant, and within 15 calendar days after contact changes; verification of the email (unique code, affirmative response) or of the phone; no response means manual verification or suspension for the registrant, manual verification only for a different account holder. Bounces or other evidence of bad data trigger re-verification with a 15-calendar-day clock, and willful inaccuracy or non-response for over 15 calendar days requires suspension or clientHold + clientTransferProhibited. The Registration Data Reminder Policy adds an annual reminder with a copy of the data and a false-data warning. Registrants must update within 7 days of a change (RAA 3.7.7.1).

### 6.5 Privacy/proxy [F27-F28]
Until ICANN's P/P accreditation program exists (implementation on hold since 2019, IRT re-convened 13 Jun 2024), registrars must require affiliates and resellers to follow the P/P Specification if they offer such services. Mosshatch can avoid the whole set by not offering a privacy/proxy product in Phase 0, because the Registration Data Policy already limits what RDAP publishes (F16). If a proxy/privacy service is ever added, the provider must publish terms, pricing, abuse and infringement contacts and its reveal/relay/termination procedures.

### 6.6 Abuse contact and DNS Abuse (April 2024) [F24-F26]
RAA 3.18 (restated 5 Apr 2024) obliges the registrar to publish an abuse email/webform on its home page, confirm receipt, investigate promptly, and promptly take appropriate mitigation on actionable evidence of DNS Abuse (malware, botnets, phishing, pharming, spam as delivery vector). ICANN's advisory: no fixed time for 'prompt', examples of 2 and 3 business days, suspension is not always the right action (compromised sites), and if the registrar delegates investigation to a reseller it must still explain and evidence the reseller's actions. Reseller duties are therefore contractual: intake, forwarding, action on instruction, receipt confirmation, and records kept up to 2 years.

### 6.7 Retention [F18-F19]
RAA 3.4 and the Data Retention Specification: registration data for the life of sponsorship plus 2 years; payment references, source-IP/HTTP-header and session logs for 180 days; registration records and correspondence for the Term plus 2 years (through the registrar 'or by its agent(s)'); TDRP data 15 months under RDP s12. A waiver route exists where local law makes retention unlawful (legal opinion or regulator ruling). 

### 6.8 Registrants' Benefits and Responsibilities [F05, F29]
Must be published or linked by resellers (RAA 3.12.7). It gives registrants rights to know the registrar's identity, terms, pricing, support and dispute routes and freedom from deceptive notices or hidden fees, and obliges them to give accurate data and reply to their registrar within 15 days.

### 6.9 UDRP and URS [F32-F33]
Registrars must comply with the UDRP and URS (RAA 3.8). On a UDRP provider's verification request the registrar has 2 business days to provide data and apply a Lock (which at minimum blocks changes to registrant and registrar information), must not tell the respondent before the Lock is applied, and implements a cancel/transfer decision after 10 business days unless a lawsuit is filed. URS is a registry-level rights protection mechanism required by new-gTLD registry agreements (.dev, .app, .studio); the .com agreement text does not mention it.

### 6.10 Sanctions and OFAC [F37-F40, F53]
ICANN does not make OFAC compliance part of the RAA, but registrars and Mosshatch (if a U.S. person, which depends on its jurisdiction) must follow OFAC law directly. The Iran communications general licence (31 CFR 560.540) does not authorise domain registration for the Government of Iran or any blocked person. OFAC expects a risk-based compliance program with five components, and its live program list shows several active comprehensive and targeted programs, updated as recently as September 2026.

### 6.11 Privacy law for a business holding registrant data [F41-F48]
GDPR applies to Mosshatch as a non-EU controller offering services to people in the EU; direct collection is not a 'transfer' but disclosure to the registrar/registry is, so Chapter V mechanisms are needed (adequacy such as the EU-US DPF, or SCCs; the DPF survived the General Court challenge on 3 Sep 2025 but is on appeal). UK GDPR needs the IDTA or the UK Addendum plus a transfer risk assessment. CCPA applies only above $26.625m revenue or 100,000 California consumers/households or 50% revenue from selling/sharing data. NIS2 expressly names resellers as 'entities providing domain name registration services', applies regardless of size, and requires accurate data, verification policies, public non-personal data and 72-hour replies to legitimate access requests, with an EU representative for non-EU providers.

## 7. Design implications for the Mosshatch plan

1. Separate three identities in the data model: Account Holder (payer/controller of the account), Registered Name Holder (the legal person on the registration agreement) and AI-agent principals; agents may propose and prepare but never accept the registration agreement, verify contact data, or approve spend (passkey-authenticated human does).
2. Collect exactly the Registration Data Policy s6 fields (name, street, city, state/province, postal code, country, phone, email; optional organization and tech contact with the s6.3/6.6 notices); no admin, billing or fax fields; phone is mandatory even if verification is by email.
3. Build a verification state machine per domain: unverified -> code emailed -> verified | day-15 breach -> manual verify or clientHold via upstream; re-run on inbound transfer, change of registrant, bounced mail and every contact edit; store proof of each step.
4. Keep an append-only registration ledger (data versions, accepted terms version, notices sent, verification, renewal reminders, abuse actions, correspondence) with retention timers by class (sponsorship + 2 years; TDRP set 15 months; logs 180 days) and legal-hold support; reconcile with GDPR erasure via documented exceptions or an ICANN waiver.
5. Build lifecycle mail and pages required by ERRP/RAA: 1-month (26-35 day) and 1-week (4-10 day) expiry notices, post-expiry notice within 5 days, DNS interruption after expiry, redemption right, deletion/auto-renewal policy at checkout, and renewal + restore fees shown on the site.
6. Ship a public abuse intake (home-page link, web form + email), automatic receipt confirmation, a case ledger, an escalation path to the upstream and an API path to request clientHold/lock; treat the ICANN advisory examples (2 and 3 business days) as internal targets; add velocity limits and anomaly checks for agent-driven bulk registration.
7. Implement registrar-directed dispute locks (UDRP/URS): freeze contact edits and transfer-out within the registrar's 2-business-day window and surface the state to humans and agents.
8. Do not run WHOIS/RDAP; choose an upstream that applies global redaction and show 'Registrar of record' (name, IANA ID, lookup.icann.org link), the ICANN education link and the Registrants' Benefits and Responsibilities text in footer and checkout; make no ICANN-accreditation claims.
9. Screen registrant name/organization/country against OFAC lists at signup, contact change and payment; maintain the blocked-jurisdiction list from OFAC's program list and counsel's view instead of hard-coding; log every screening decision.
10. Treat registrant contact data as its own regulated data class: separate tables, KMS-backed field encryption, access logging and least-privilege agent tokens; never co-mingle with the encrypted secrets vault.
11. Adopt per-TLD policy profiles: .com (Verisign RRA terms), .dev/.app/.studio (URS, Spec 11 abuse clause), .ai (registry rules, UDRP, RDAP), .io (own dispute policy, no IANA-listed RDAP, Chagos risk); do not assume the Registration Data Policy applies to ccTLDs.
12. Prepare privacy paperwork before launch: data map/RoPA, notice naming registrar, registry and escrow as recipients, Art 28 DPAs with Neon, Vercel, Stripe, Resend and the KMS provider, SCCs + UK Addendum with the registrar, EU/UK representative, 72-hour breach runbook, and a yearly CCPA threshold check.
13. Charge the card before sending EPP create (RAA 3.7.4 payment assurance) and design refunds/chargebacks so a disputed payment never silently re-activates or deletes a name.
14. Add a quarterly compliance-diff task: ICANN Consensus Policies page, RAA/global amendments, Transfer Policy, P/P program, OFAC lists, DPF appeal, upstream notices.
