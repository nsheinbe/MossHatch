### E. Design-relevant facts

25. F61 zone-file access: CORRECTED. The new-gTLD terms are as stated (.studio agreement Spec 4 2.1.3 "no more than once per 24 hour period"; 2.1.5 lawful purposes except to "(ii) enable high volume, automated, electronic processes that send queries or data to the systems of Registry Operator or any ICANN-accredited registrar"; 2.1.6 "not less than three (3) months"; 2.1.7 no fee). But the dossier says ".com ... not covered by this clause" and lists .com zone access as unverified: the current .com Registry Agreement Appendix 3 ("Zone File Access") gives the same CZDA-provider-mediated access, "no more than once per 24 hour period", grants "not less than three (3) months", no fee, with an even broader use restriction (adds marketing and "interrupt, disrupt or interfere in the normal business operations of any registrant"). So .com is covered contractually; only .ai/.io (ccTLDs) remain unverified. Also note the clause forbids using zone data to drive high-volume automated queries to registrars, so a zone-derived name list must not feed bulk registrar checks. Also, held names (qvz.com) are not in a zone, so a zone copy shares DNS's blind spot.

28. F36-F38 third-party resolver logging and limits: CONFIRMED with omissions. Cloudflare: "delete the Public Resolver Logs within 25 hours", truncated source IPs deleted within 25 hours. Omitted by the dossier: (a) Google's documented default rate limit is 1500 QPS per client IPv4 (or IPv6 /64) on the very ISP page the analyst cited, so a type-ahead will never hit Google's DoH throttle; (b) Cloudflare's public pledge "will not sell or share Public Resolver users' personal data with third parties" (shares limited anonymised logs with APNIC) and third-party audit. SAC 022's DNS-operator vector is real in principle, but these two operators publicly commit against it; the design advice to avoid DoH for user-typed names is still defensible on data-minimisation grounds, but it should not imply these operators are known to monetise queries.

## Things the analyst missed or that change emphasis

- Numeric Google Public DNS limit exists (1500 QPS per client IP). Cloudflare no-sale/no-share pledge and APNIC sharing omitted (item 28).
- .com IS covered by CZDA zone-file terms (item 25).
- Operative RAA is the 21 Jan 2024 text (global amendment 5 Apr 2024); Registration Data Policy 15-month TDRP retention rule (items 23-24).
- 2026 Base RA (Board approval 12 Mar 2026) keeps the registry anti-front-running clause: positive recent change, no loosening (item 22).
- IETF individual draft draft-newton-regext-rdap-domain-availability-00 (ARIN/DENIC, Dec 2016, expired 19 Jun 2017, "a minimal profile of RDAP which can be used to check the availability of domain names") never became an RFC, so there is no standard RDAP availability service; further evidence that RDAP availability is ad hoc.
- Premium-tier data is visible in the registrar price channel (item 27).
- The McElroy complaint also named ICANN (item 19).
- INCIDENTAL, for the parent project (not part of this topic): Verisign RDAP shows `mosshatch.com` registered on 2026-09-29T17:48:38Z (expires 2027-09-29, status client transfer prohibited, nameservers DNS1/2.REGISTRAR-SERVERS.COM, i.e. a Namecheap-default DNS setup), and the Vercel registrar reports it unavailable, while mosshatch.dev/.app/.io/.studio are available at $9.99/$9.99/$14.99/$21.99 first year and .ai at $160 for 2 years.
