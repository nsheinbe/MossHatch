# Mosshatch decisions

One entry per decision: context, choice, why, and what would change it, plus a status. Status values: **Needs your decision** (listed in `PLAN.md` under Open decisions with a recommendation; where it departs from the brief, the status says so), **Proposed default** (I decided because a defensible default exists; veto it in `PLAN.md` under Assumptions, and the table "Where to veto" at the end of this file names the assumption that covers each one), **Fixed by the brief** (recorded for completeness) and **Recorded** (a fact about how the work was done, with no choice to make). An entry that mixes a fixed part and a proposed part says so in its status (D-018). Evidence for every entry lives in `docs/research/`; a Sources line gives full URLs, all accessed 2026-09-29. A number chosen here rather than found in a source is labelled "own target". Dates are 2026-09-29 unless stated. In these entries a "project environment" is a customer's `dev`, `preview` or `prod` set of variables, and a "deployment environment" is one of Mosshatch's own (local, preview, staging, production).

## D-001 Registrar: OpenSRS primary, CentralNic Reseller fallback
**Status:** Needs your decision.
**Context.** The brief launches as a reseller and asks for a live-verified comparison. Nine providers were researched in depth and nine more screened; the registrar dossiers were followed by independent verification passes (`docs/research/reg-*.md`, `verification-digest.md`). Two of the brief's five candidates are only partly verified: Namecheap (retail prices only, terms unread) and ResellerClub (its site was unreachable).
**Choice.** Primary: OpenSRS (Tucows Domains Inc., IANA 69) at the Essential tier. Fallback: CentralNic Reseller (Key-Systems GmbH, IANA 269), for new registrations and per-extension routing only; it is not a failover for domains already at OpenSRS, which can leave only by ICANN transfer with each registrant's code and approval. It counts as a real fallback only after: a written price quote for the other five extensions (if that is worse than Dynadot's list prices, Dynadot becomes the fallback once its ToU s3.1(d) conflict is resolved in writing); written confirmation or waiver of its stated qualification of 100 registered or transferred domains within a year; counsel's read of Reseller Schedule 3.6 (liability for all fees whether or not customers pay) and MSA 13.7 (every domain transferred out before the end of the term); and written permission before EU individuals are admitted (its sign-up attestation excludes them, which the US-only scope of D-006 meets). Its default account renewal mode is AutoRenew, so it is switched off to match D-008. Dynadot is the cost benchmark. Ship `MockRegistrar` first; the first real adapter is OpenSRS.
**Why.** OpenSRS is the only candidate whose published contract is written for resale, needs no ICANN accreditation, publishes wholesale prices for all six extensions and has a sandbox; sign-up is documented as automated and self-serve, though a sales gate cannot be excluded. Its costs are operational: an XML API, an IP allow-list, no idempotency key, and DNS through `SET_DNS_ZONE` (no CAA, no TTL; the documentation does not say whether it replaces the whole zone or only the record types supplied, so the adapter reads, modifies and writes under a per-domain lock and tests this before Phase 3). Its contract risks are for counsel before signing: a one-way indemnity with no stated cap (MSA 22.1), a sole remedy of termination and return of unused deposit (21.6), suspension if Tucows is threatened with suit and Mosshatch gives no written assurance of its indemnity (22.2), a right to require minimum order levels (12.8), a USD 35 fee per chargeback or disputed deposit (12.10), and a prepaid balance whose going negative is a material breach (12.1). The USD 95 activation fee converts to account credit, so it is prepaid wholesale and not a running cost.
**Would change if.** OpenSRS answers the written questions in PLAN 4.1 badly (lookup limits and fee, add-grace refund rules, duplicate-registration behaviour, `.ai` and `.io` handling, opt-out from parked pages, whether the Manage Web Interface can be switched off, whether Horizon credentials are issued before the activation fee); counsel finds the MSA unacceptable (broad suspension and price-change rights, the indemnity, the sole-remedy clause, the assignment bar); or CentralNic or Dynadot quotes materially lower wholesale with acceptable paper.
**Sources.** https://assets.opensrs.com/Uploads/Master-Services-Agreement.pdf, https://opensrs.com/domains/pricing, https://opensrs.com/payment-terms (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-002 Payment path: authorize, register, then capture
**Status:** Needs your decision (departs from the brief).
**Context.** The brief registers only after a verified `payment_intent.succeeded`, with a refund path on failure. Stripe keeps its processing fee on refunds, but cancelling an uncaptured authorization is free, and issuers are not required to report an authorization as possible fraud, as they are for a captured payment.
**Choice.** Hosted Checkout with `payment_intent_data.capture_method=manual`, cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay only (ACH disabled, Link off until its capture window is documented). Register while the money is held; capture on success; cancel the authorization on failure. Renewals are charged first (off-session, immediate capture) and renewed upstream second. Transfers-in are captured at submission and refunded on failure, because an inbound transfer can take about two weeks, longer than any authorization.
**Why.** Registration is the step that can fail; failing it should cost nothing. It also removes a class of refund-fee losses and dispute-eligible captures. A customer-initiated Checkout authorization lasts seven days on the four card brands, but `capture_before` is null for some cards and absent for Link, so the working deadline is the authorization time plus four days (PLAN 4.3b sets the guards).
**Open legal question.** RAA 3.7.4 bars the accredited registrar (Tucows) from activating a name until it is satisfied that it has received a reasonable assurance of payment of its registration fee. A charge to a credit card, or another mechanism giving a similar level of assurance, is enough "provided that the obligation to pay becomes final and non-revocable by the Registered Name Holder upon activation of the registration". Tucows is paid from Mosshatch's prepaid OpenSRS balance, and RAA 3.12 makes the registrar answer to ICANN for its resellers and requires a written flow-down, so whether this duty reaches Mosshatch is a counsel question, as is whether an approved but uncaptured authorization, captured on success, meets the proviso. The registrar-data dossier reads the rule as requiring the charge before the registry create; the Stripe dossiers recommend an authorization hold. Counsel questions: PLAN 4.7 question 3. No card-network guidance on a high ratio of cancelled authorizations was found.
**Would change if.** Counsel says a hold is not enough (then capture first and refund on failure, accepting the lost Stripe fee on each failed registration: about USD 0.86 on a USD 19.25 `.com` order at 2.9% plus USD 0.30, our own arithmetic, more on international cards or with Stripe Tax); sandbox tests show manual capture misbehaves with a supported wallet or with Stripe Tax in Checkout; authorization windows prove too short for slow registrations; or a required payment method cannot do manual capture.
**Sources.** https://itp.cdn.icann.org/en/files/accredited-registrars/registrar-accreditation-agreement-21jan24-en.htm (RAA 3.7.4, 3.12), https://docs.stripe.com/payments/place-a-hold-on-a-payment-method, https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/monitoring-programs, https://stripe.com/pricing (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-003 Fee structure and price presentation
**Status:** Needs your decision (departs from the brief's single flat fee: three levels).
**Context.** The brief's USD 1.50 placeholder is at or just above zero-contribution break-even on a first order for `.com`, `.dev` and `.app` (break-even fees 1.25, 1.25 and 1.50; on renewals `.app` needs 1.75), but it earns only USD 0.09 to 0.31 per domain-year there (our arithmetic from the model's F=2.00 and F=3.00 columns), and it loses money on `.studio` (-1.04), `.io` (-2.99) and `.ai` (-6.02). Stripe's 2.9% scales with ticket size, so one flat number cannot serve both a USD 15 and a USD 222 order.
**Choice.** USD 4.00 per domain-year where wholesale is under USD 50 a year (`.com`, `.dev`, `.app`); USD 9.00 from USD 50 to under USD 100 (`.studio`, `.io`); USD 10.00 from USD 100 (`.ai`). The same fee applies on registration, renewal, transfer-in and restore. First-order prices at November 2026 wholesale: `.com` 19.25, `.dev` 21.00, `.app` 25.00, `.studio` 60.00, `.io` 69.00, and `.ai` 242.00 for the mandatory two years (121.00 a year). No promotional first-year prices; "renews at" is read from the registrar's renewal price and both prices display at the same size. The Hatch sheet shows registry and registrar cost plus the Mosshatch fee (D-024); if OpenSRS promotional first-year prices apply to API orders, the sheet shows the amount OpenSRS actually charged or the promotion is declined, so the breakdown is true. `.ai` shows "First 2 years (minimum)". Registry-premium names are not sold (D-031).
**Why.** The fee needed for +USD 1.50 expected contribution per domain-year on a first order is 3.00 (`.com`, `.dev`), 3.25 (`.app`), 4.25 (`.studio`), 6.50 (`.io`) and 9.75 (`.ai`). The chosen fees give +2.62 (`.com`), +2.55 (`.dev`), +2.40 (`.app`), +5.91 (`.studio`), +3.95 (`.io`) and +1.85 (`.ai`) per domain-year, so every extension clears that line; `.studio` is over-recovered by design because each level is a single threshold. `.ai` clears it narrowly: it falls to +0.35 or +0.32 when disputes reach 2.0% and 3.0% or every card is international, to -4.24 with 8% first-order refunds (the model treats `.ai` and `.io` wholesale as not recovered on a refund) and to -1.55 if wholesale deposits are funded by card, which is one more reason `.ai` stays gated (D-006). Mosshatch will not be the cheapest on `.com` (USD 19.25 against about USD 11 elsewhere). The only source of numbers is `docs/research/unit-economics-output.md`.
**Would change if.** OpenSRS volume tiers or a cheaper provider lower wholesale; real dispute and refund rates differ from the assumptions (the model's softest inputs); or you prefer a single fee and fewer extensions (a USD 4 single fee works for `.com`, `.dev`, `.app` only).
**Sources.** https://opensrs.com/domains/pricing, https://opensrs.com/domains/tld-price-changes (`.com` 15.25 from 2026-11-01, `.studio` 51 from 2026-10-06), https://stripe.com/pricing (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-004 Trust model of the Nest and choice of KMS
**Status:** Needs your decision.
**Context.** The brief requires a per-secret AES-256-GCM data key wrapped by a cloud KMS key. Agents, the CLI and wire-it recipes need server-side decryption, so the vault cannot be zero-knowledge.
**Choice.** AWS KMS in us-east-1 through Vercel OIDC federation, with no static cloud keys. One set of keys per Mosshatch deployment environment (production, staging, development). Inside production there are two keys and two IAM roles: `vault-prod` for customers' `prod` secrets and `vault-nonprod` for their `dev` and `preview` secrets, so four keys in all. The encryption context `{app, env, owner_id, secret_id}` carries the customer's project environment in `env`, and the key policy requires the matching key. Both production roles trust the same OIDC subject, so the split guards against logic bugs and scope confusion, not against code execution in `web`. The trust policy is pinned to the Vercel Team issuer, the production subject and a custom audience (PLAN 4.3b Secrets). Four keys cost about USD 4 a month, about USD 12 by year three with rotation. The vault module lives in the `web` project (D-005 topology); moving the Decrypt-capable identity to its own small project is a Phase 4 option recorded under D-026. A client-held-key tier (passkey PRF) is recorded as a later hardening option for production secrets, not built. Honest limits, repeated in the Nest help text: anyone who can run code in the production project can decrypt what the service can decrypt until the key is disabled; Vercel (TLS termination, function memory) and AWS (data keys in KMS) can technically observe plaintext in normal operation; a value on screen can be seen by extensions, clipboard managers and screen capture; a value returned to an agent or printed by the CLI leaves Mosshatch's control; deleted secrets stay in encrypted database history up to the retention window; losing the key loses every secret (D-026). Product copy and the terms must not say that staff cannot read secrets; any statement about who can read them must match PLAN 4.3b.
**Why.** Only AWS documents IAM and key-policy conditions on the encryption context and has a server-side re-wrap (`ReEncrypt`). Cost is USD 1 per key-month plus USD 0.03 per 10,000 requests, so about USD 4 to 12 a month for four keys at 1,000 secrets. Server-side decryption is what lets agents, the CLI and recipes work at all.
**Would change if.** You want a zero-knowledge vault (which breaks agents, the CLI and recipes for those secrets), or measured KMS latency from Vercel is unacceptable.
**Sources.** https://docs.aws.amazon.com/kms/latest/developerguide/encrypt_context.html, https://aws.amazon.com/kms/pricing/, https://vercel.com/docs/oidc/aws (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-005 Egress to the registrar
**Status:** Needs your decision (needed before the live rehearsal against OpenSRS production, not before Phases 1 to 3).
**Context.** OpenSRS accepts live API calls only from allow-listed addresses: five rules by default, more on request to Support, and a change can take up to 15 minutes to apply. The Horizon sandbox needs no allow-list. Vercel has no fixed egress by default. Vercel Static IPs are one address pair per region, shared with a small group of other customers, apply to every deployment environment of the project (Preview included), and cost USD 100 a month per project plus Private Data Transfer at regional rates (from USD 0.15 per GB) on all outbound traffic of that project, builds included. CentralNic's allow-list is optional and off by default.
**Choice.** Recommended: Vercel Static IPs on the `registrar` project only, the one project that holds registrar credentials; `web` calls it through an internal signed RPC (topology in PLAN 4.3b). The allow-list is a narrowing control, not a second factor. The registrar key is a Sensitive, Production-only variable; Preview and Development use the Horizon key. `registrar` accepts only the commands the adapter needs, validates their arguments and enforces a velocity fuse across all customers (own targets in PLAN 4.3b Egress). Stronger option: a small self-run gateway on a dedicated address that alone holds the registrar credentials and enforces the same commands and fuse outside Vercel, so the credentials are never in Vercel, at the cost of one more component to operate. Vercel Secure Compute (Enterprise) is another route to a dedicated address.
**Why.** Static IPs add nothing to operate and narrow where a stolen key can be used from. They are not a second factor: the addresses are shared, the OpenSRS key is one reseller-wide key that cannot be scoped, and code running in the `registrar` project holds it from an allow-listed address. Key hygiene therefore stays the main control: rotate on any suspicion, and detect through the unattributed-change detector (PLAN 4.6, registrar-account row), which compares the registrar's view with committed `actions` rows, plus alerts on unexplained balance movement. Mosshatch cannot alert on reads it did not make.
**Would change if.** USD 100 a month plus data transfer is too high at launch volume (a gateway costs about USD 6 a month, our own estimate, plus the work of operating it); you want the allow-list to count as a real control (then the gateway or Secure Compute); or the chosen provider needs no allow-list.
**Sources.** https://vercel.com/docs/networking/static-ips, https://vercel.com/pricing, https://support.opensrs.com/support/solutions/articles/201000063062 (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-006 Launch scope
**Status:** Needs your decision.
**Context.** Sales tax and VAT registration, the EU accessibility act, and several consumer-law regimes are cheaper to satisfy in one jurisdiction. `.ai` and `.io` have unresolved product and legal issues.
**Choice.** United States billing addresses only (all others blocked at checkout until registered), USD only, cards and wallets only. Sell `.com`, `.dev`, `.app` first; `.studio` after fee approval (D-003); `.io` and `.ai` only after per-extension tests and disclosures. The mock and the creatures support all six from Phase 1.
**Why.** `.ai` needs a two-year minimum charge, offers no privacy service and shows contacts publicly, is absent from the OpenSRS sandbox, and its transfer rules are registry-specific. `.io` has no privacy service and its auth codes are issued by OpenSRS support. Its future is uncertain: the UK-Mauritius treaty signed 2025-05-22 is not in force; UK ratification legislation lapsed in April 2026, and on 22-23 September 2026 ministers said the deal will be reworked while payments stay on hold pending US support (AFP, Guardian, BBC). The direction is stall, so near-term loss is unlikely and the question stays open; any retirement would run five years (maximum ten). "Shelved after the US withdrew support" rests on a headline the verifier did not open. Separately, an unrelated provider's sandbox (Porkbun) returned an undocumented FRAUD_BLOCK on every `.io` create; the cause is unknown and may be a sandbox artifact, so it is not evidence about OpenSRS, but it is one more reason to test `.io` end to end before selling it.
**Would change if.** Tests and counsel clear `.ai` and `.io`; a tax adviser recommends other jurisdictions; you want EU customers at launch (VAT One-Stop-Shop registration, EU consumer-law and accessibility-act review).
**Sources.** https://www.icann.org/en/blogs/details/the-chagos-archipelago-and-the-io-domain-14-11-2024-en, https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/uk-says-will-revisit-chagos-deal-after-latest-trump-criticism-3-1.500685166, https://support.opensrs.com/support/solutions/articles/201000068801--ai-domain-policies (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-008 Renewal and auto-renew policy
**Status:** Proposed default (veto in Assumptions).
**Context.** The brief bans pre-checked boxes and surprise bills. Renewals touch California, New York, Vermont and Virginia renewal-notice law, Visa's stored-credential rules, ICANN's Expired Registration Recovery Policy (ERRP) and the upstream's prepaid balance.
**Choice.** Auto-renew is opt-in, unchecked, a passkey-signed mandate with a price ceiling (`mandate.sign`, not held after recovery so renewals keep working). The charge is on day C = E-10, ten days before expiry E. Notices go at E-43, E-32, C-8 (= E-18) and E+1; the ICANN expiry notices are sent regardless. A price-change reminder goes 21 days before the charge (inside California's 7 to 30 day window and New York's window). A change to Mosshatch's own flat fee is announced at least 60 days ahead as an informational notice; a wholesale change that affects a renewal is emailed as soon as known and repeated 21 days before the charge. The decline ladder runs at the charge, +3 and +6 days, with at least seven days to pay by other means. Registrar-side auto-renew stays off. Registrations are refundable for the shorter of five days and the upstream window; `.io` and `.ai` are shown as non-refundable before payment. Unpaid domains are never locked or suspended as leverage. Gandi's contract forbids it; the ICANN Transfer Policy bars denying a transfer for non-payment of a pending or future period (I.A.3.9.1), bars withholding the code over a payment dispute (I.A.5.4) and bars using transfers to secure payment (I.A.3.10). I.A.3.7.3 still lets the registrar of record deny a transfer, with Registrar Hold, for unpaid earlier or current periods including charge-backs; only Tucows can apply that, and Mosshatch would ask for it only for a charge-back on the registration itself, pending counsel. This is one policy, stated once in PLAN 4.2 and referenced from 4.1 and COMPLIANCE C-33.
**Why.** It satisfies the brief (no pre-checked boxes), California, New York and Vermont renewal-notice laws, Visa's stored-credential rules and ICANN's ERRP. The first draft's cadence had zero margin against Visa's seven-day floor and California's 15 to 45 day window, so the verified cadence adds margin.
**Would change if.** Counsel reads the auto-renewed-name ERRP notice rule differently (a charged renewal at E-10 moves the expiry before the ERRP notice that falls 4 to 10 days before expiry).
**Sources.** https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17602, https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/consensus-policies/expired-registration-recovery-policy/expired-registration-recovery-policy-21-02-2024-en, https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/accredited-registrars/resources/domain-name-transfers/policy (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-009 Step-up scope and the Gate
**Status:** Needs your decision (departs from the brief).
**Context.** The brief puts unlock, transfer and nameserver changes behind a passkey and asks the Gate for a cooling-off explanation and "cancel any time". OpenSRS has no API to start, approve or cancel an outbound transfer: the gaining registrar starts it, OpenSRS emails the registrant a link to approve or decline, and silence for five days approves; `.io` codes are issued by OpenSRS support. ICANN Transfer Policy I.A.5.3 bars an unlock or code mechanism more restrictive than the one used for contact or nameserver changes.
**Choice.** Thirteen step-up action ids, listed in PLAN 4.5: the brief's `secret.reveal`, `domain.nameservers.change`, `domain.unlock`, `domain.transfer_out` (unlock and code issue in one step-up), `agent.purchase.approve`, `agent.token.create` and `agent.token.widen`, plus `domain.contact.change` (parity with I.A.5.3), `dns.sensitive.approve` (D-011), `device.approve`, `passkey.add`, `card.publish` and `mandate.sign`. After a passkey step-up, unlock and the transfer authorization code are available immediately. A security delay is an opt-in setting the owner turns on in advance ("lock harder", default 48 hours, always inside five calendar days); it also covers contact and nameserver changes, so it is never more restrictive than they are. Every unlock, code issue and nameserver change sends a notification with a freeze link. The brief's "cooling-off" is therefore an owner-chosen delay, not a default. The Gate can unlock, issue and show the code, and derive Traveling from `GET_TRANSFERS_AWAY`; "cancel any time" holds until the code is used (re-lock and issue a new code) and afterwards means declining in OpenSRS's email, which the panel says plainly. OpenSRS's end-user Manage Web Interface is a second control plane, because registrants can see and change the auth code there: it is switched off for the reseller before the first live registration or, if OpenSRS refuses, auth codes are hidden and password resets blocked (a written question to OpenSRS). Mosshatch issues its own code at each unlock, re-randomises it at re-lock and after 24 hours, and never reads back a stored one. Transfer timing copy is in D-037.
**Why.** ICANN's five-calendar-day duty in I.A.5.1 and I.A.5.2 applies only where the registrar offers no self-service, so it binds for `.io` (OpenSRS support issues the code) and not for a self-service Gate. With self-service the limits are I.A.5.3, I.A.5.4, I.A.3.10 and the five-day pending window once a transfer starts. Whether a default delay is allowed is an open counsel question (PLAN 4.7 question 2), so the conservative design is a delay the holder requests, capped at five calendar days.
**Would change if.** Counsel approves a default delay; or OpenSRS offers a way for a reseller to approve, decline or place a hold on an outbound transfer, in which case the passkey can gate that too.
**Sources.** https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/accredited-registrars/resources/domain-name-transfers/policy, https://support.opensrs.com/support/solutions/articles/201000063132-transfer-away-process, https://support.opensrs.com/support/solutions/articles/201000063434 (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-010 Hold-to-reveal accessibility
**Status:** Needs your decision (departs from the brief).
**Context.** The brief's Nest specifies hold-to-reveal (press and hold, or hold Space or Enter), a passkey step-up and an automatic re-hide after 10 seconds.
**Choice.** Ship hold-to-reveal as an enhancement, and always provide a single-activation path (activate, confirm, reveal) for keyboard, switch and voice users. The 10-second re-hide is the default and is adjustable up to ten times that; the value is never in a live region.
**Why.** WCAG's Understanding text for 2.1.1 lists "a key must be held down for an extended period" among the specific timings, so the brief's "hold Space/Enter" would not pass an audit. The automatic re-hide is a time limit under 2.2.1, hence the adjustment.
**Would change if.** You accept the audit risk (not recommended).
**Sources.** https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-011 Sensitive DNS changes by agents and recipes
**Status:** Proposed default.
**Context.** The brief gates nameserver changes and agent purchases behind a passkey but says nothing about record-level changes. An MX change intercepts email, including password resets at other services, and agents and recipes act through scoped tokens or stored provider credentials.
**Choice.** A change to `MX`, `NS`, `DS`, apex `A/AAAA/CNAME` or SPF/DMARC `TXT` records made by an agent or a recipe creates a pending approval that only a human passkey (`dns.sensitive.approve`, held after recovery) can complete, like a purchase. The approval binds a hash of the previewed change set (the normalised records before and after). Who else is asked, and which further record classes count, is D-034.
**Why.** An MX change is as dangerous as a nameserver change, which the brief already gates.
**Would change if.** It makes recipes feel heavy: the record list can then shrink, but a plain click in a signed-in session never counts as the approval.

## D-012 Names, trademarks and hatchkind.com
**Status:** Needs your decision (counsel).
**Context.** The brief asks for a trademark knockout search on both names and a proposal for `hatchkind.com` as the public card host. The knockout search found no identical MOSSHATCH or HATCHKIND mark. CROSSHATCH (US Reg. 7836851, Class 42, also WO 1766322 designating the EU) is the closest collision; crosshatch.io says "We shut down July 2025" but does not name the registrant, so linking it to the registrant, Olympus Technologies Inc., is our inference. HATCH is crowded (HATCH LABS; Hatch Ltd. has pending EU and UK filings), and the Python project manager Hatch and its hatchling build backend are a developer-tool collision for HATCHKIND.
**Choice.** Keep both names. Instruct a trademark attorney to run a full clearance (suggested scope: classes 9, 35, 42 and 45 in the US, EU and UK, starting from CROSSHATCH and HATCH LABS). Use `hatchkind.com` as the public card host under the conditions in PLAN 4.8 (credential-free, cookie-free, read-only, publish-gated, no email), with the host as a configuration value. Publishing a card is a passkey step-up (`card.publish`) behind a Web Risk check and a typosquat and homoglyph screen; indexing is D-035. Do not build public brand equity in HATCHKIND until cleared.
**Why.** A knockout search only finds obvious collisions; it cannot clear a mark. The CROSSHATCH registration is live and the HATCH field is crowded, so a wrong guess costs a rebrand after users exist.
**Would change if.** Clearance finds a conflict; a rename then becomes a redirect.
**Sources.** https://crosshatch.io/, https://branddb.wipo.int/, https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/, https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/mosshatch.com (accessed 2026-09-29).


## D-013 Toolchain and versions

## D-015 Content Security Policy, cookies and headers
**Status:** Proposed default.
**Context.** Brief section 3 requires a strict CSP, HttpOnly SameSite session cookies and no third-party scripts beyond fonts, and section 9 requires the Stripe redirect to work.
**Choice.** The enforced CSP and the response-header set are the "Strict CSP" and "Response headers" rows of PLAN 4.3a. ## D-016 Sessions and recovery parameters
**Status:** Proposed default.
**Context.** The brief makes passkeys the only sign-in (no passwords in v1), so account recovery is the path an attacker is most likely to target.
**Choice.** 15-minute idle and 8-hour absolute sessions. Step-up challenges live 120 seconds and are single use (own target: the research recommended 300 seconds; the WebAuthn `timeout` is 120000 ms, and an expired prompt offers Try again with a new challenge, so nobody is forced to finish inside the window, WCAG 2.2.1). Recovery uses two credentials, ten recovery codes, an emailed code, and a slow email-only path. Holds apply to the actions marked held in the PLAN 4.5 step-up table: after recovery by codes plus an emailed code, 24 hours; after email-only recovery, a 72-hour cooling-off and then a 72-hour hold (the research proposed seven days; 72 hours keeps unlock and code release inside ICANN's five calendar days, counsel to confirm); after a passkey is added while signed in, 24 hours on `secret.reveal` only. Notices about recovery, unlock and code issue go to every verified notification address (the login email, a second address on a different mail domain, and the registrant email when it differs), and a second address or a downloaded recovery code is required before the first domain purchase or stored secret, because the login mailbox alone is not an independent channel. An opt-in hardened mode covers device-bound credentials.
**Why.** NIST SP 800-63B-4 AAL2 is met (it says an overall session SHOULD be at most 24 hours and idle at most 1 hour). 15 minutes idle is the bottom of OWASP's 15 to 30 minute range for low-risk applications, and 8 hours is the top of its 4 to 8 hour range for a work-day application. OWASP's 2 to 5 minute idle range for high-value applications is not met by the session; a per-action passkey step-up covers it instead. The first draft's 30 minutes and 12 hours were looser than the sources it cited. The hold lengths are own targets.
**Would change if.** Usability testing shows the idle timeout is too aggressive for the Nest (only that surface could be relaxed), or counsel says the recovery holds conflict with the Transfer Policy (PLAN 4.7 question 2).
**Sources.** https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html, https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-019 Agent surface
**Status:** Proposed default (departs from non-negotiable 2 for bearer reads of secrets; veto in Assumptions).
**Context.** Brief section 3 requires an MCP server and REST that use the same scoped tokens, and a CLI whose `pull` and `run` read secrets; non-negotiable 2 asks for a passkey step-up before revealing a secret.
**Choice.** MCP specification 2026-07-28 on `@modelcontextprotocol/server` 2.x (stateless Streamable HTTP), static scoped bearer tokens first (`mh_<kind>_<32 base62>+CRC32`, SHA-256 at rest) and an OAuth authorization server in Phase 5; server-enforced approval; no secret-returning tool marked read-only. Only Claude Code is documented to speak 2026-07-28; Claude apps, Cursor and VS Code are unverified, so the server keeps the SDK's default `legacy: 'stateless'` for 2025-era clients and does not set `legacy: 'reject'` until each client is tested in Phase 5. **Two secret read paths, for you to veto.** (1) Web session: every reveal is a `secret.reveal` step-up, one secret per assertion. (2) Bearer binding created by step-up (`agent.token.create`, or `device.approve` for the CLI) with `secrets.read:<domain>:<env>`: it reads without a per-read ceremony, `:prod` must be named explicitly, every read is audited, rate limited by token id and summarised by email, and a missing scope is 403, never an empty list. REST: `POST /domains/{fqdn}/secrets/{env}/read` (bearer only). CLI login uses the RFC 8628 device flow with a typed-code, passkey-gated approval page that shows the scopes being granted; keychain storage via `@napi-rs/keyring` (Linux store pinned to Secret Service). `run` refuses a deny-list of dangerous variable names (for example `LD_PRELOAD`, `NODE_OPTIONS`), and the same names are refused when a secret is written, so legitimate uses such as `NODE_OPTIONS=--max-old-space-size` cannot be stored in the vault. The CLI is published only from CI with npm trusted publishing and provenance, no install scripts and look-alike package names reserved.
**Why.** Static bearer tokens work in Claude Code, Cursor and VS Code today, while claude.ai and Desktop connectors need OAuth, hence the OAuth server later. Approval is server-enforced because client-side prompts are not a control: elicitation is optional, a Claude Code hook can auto-answer it, and legacy stateless clients cannot receive it. No secret-returning tool is marked read-only because VS Code and Claude skip confirmation for read-only tools. The passkey at binding creation is the delegated step-up for the reads it authorizes; without it `pull` and `run` could not work in CI. A value returned to an MCP client is stored by that client (transcripts, tool-result files), so it counts as having left Mosshatch's control; `mosshatch run` is the preferred path because values never pass through a tool result.
**Would change if.** Major clients adopt the 2026-07-28 revision and OAuth becomes the only connection path; or you want a passkey per production pull (then production reads become browser-reveal only and `run` for production stops working in CI).
**Sources.** https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/authorization, https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8628, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp (accessed 2026-09-29).

## D-026 Loss radius and KMS key policy
**Status:** Proposed default.
**Context.** The KMS design covers compromise (the emergency stop) but not loss. If the production key is scheduled for deletion, the AWS account is suspended or us-east-1 is impaired, every customer secret is unreadable, and under pressure the emergency stop can turn into permanent loss.
**Choice.** State the loss radius plainly: losing the production key, or the AWS account that holds it, permanently destroys every customer secret; the Nest is not a backup of record and the Terms say so. Controls (PLAN 4.3b Loss radius): each production key is multi-Region with a replica in us-east-2 (USD 1 a month per replica, USD 2 for the two production keys, not in the four-key figure); the key policy denies `kms:ScheduleKeyDeletion`, `kms:DisableKey` and `kms:PutKeyPolicy` to everyone but a break-glass role that needs hardware-key MFA, and scheduled deletion always uses the 30-day window; alarms on those events and on `CreateGrant`; separate production, staging and log-archive AWS accounts, the root user on a hardware key with no access keys, and CloudTrail with log-file validation delivered to the log-archive bucket with Object Lock; a monthly drill that restores last night's off-Neon backup and decrypts a canary secret (D-027). Three further production keys (PII, MAC, backup) cost about USD 3 a month. The vault fails closed: any KMS error returns 503 and nothing decrypts without KMS. The AWS account is needed from Phase 2, not Phase 4. Two options are left for Phase 4 with their costs: moving the Decrypt-capable identity into its own small Vercel project (one more project, an authenticated internal path, duplicated authorization logic), and a second-cloud key wrap (a multi-keyring).

**Status:** Proposed default.

**Status:** Proposed default.
**Context.** The audit log is append-only and hash-chained, `users` has a `deleted_at` column, and counsel question 9 asks which retention clock applies, but nothing designed how closure, export and erasure work. The chain format, the personal-data columns and the export shape are fixed in Phase 2.

## D-034 Sensitive DNS approval scope
**Status:** Proposed default.
**Context.** D-011 puts agent and recipe changes to sensitive records behind a human passkey. A review found two gaps: a human cookie session (a stolen cookie, or script running in the page) can still change the same records with no step-up, and the record list was too narrow (DKIM keys, `_acme-challenge`, provider verification tokens, wildcards and subdomain hosts can each capture mail, certificates or cookies).
**Choice.** The classification is computed server-side (PLAN 4.3b DNS, Sensitive records): first by owner name (any label starting with an underscore, the apex, `www`, any wildcard, and the hosts `autoconfig`, `autodiscover` and `mta-sts`), then by type (MX, NS, DS, SRV, and apex A, AAAA and CNAME), with SPF, DMARC, DKIM and provider-verification TXT prefixes only as a backstop. The approval (`dns.sensitive.approve`, held after recovery) binds the hash of the previewed before and after record sets, so one passkey approves a whole recipe plan and nothing outside it. It applies only where Mosshatch hosts the DNS. Agents and recipes get a pending approval that a human completes; a human session is not gated, and that residual risk is stated in the threat model (PLAN 4.6). Closing it costs a passkey on every MX, SPF or DKIM edit by the owner.
**Why.** Gating the owner's own edits would make the dashboard feel heavy for the common case, while agents and recipes are the new actors whose mistakes or hijacking the brief worries about. Stating the gap keeps the claim honest.
**Would change if.** You want the human gap closed (then the same classification requires a step-up in every session, one per previewed plan), or the recipe research shows a record class we did not list.

## D-044 Dotenv output quoting
**Status:** Proposed default (departs from PLAN 4.5 CLI and threat row 22; decided during the build, 2026-09-30).

**Context.** PLAN 4.3b defines the AAD as the length-prefixed concatenation of `format_version`, `alg_id`, `domain_id`, `secret_id`, `name`, `env` and `version`, never the KEK ARN, so a re-wrap keeps it valid. Nothing in it tied a row to the production or the non-production key, and binding a KEK id or ARN would break `ReEncrypt` onto a new key.
**Choice.** The AAD is the RFC 8785 (JCS) canonical JSON of `{alg, domain_id, env, format_version, kek_class, kind, name, secret_id, user_id, version}` for a secret, and of the matching connection fields for a stored provider credential. It names the KEK class (`vault-prod` or `vault-nonprod`), never a key id or ARN, and is recomputed from the row at decrypt, never stored (`packages/api/src/vault/envelope.ts`).
**Why.** A re-wrap onto a new key of the same class (rotation, or the compromise drill) keeps every row valid, while a row moved between classes fails to decrypt. JCS is as unambiguous as a length prefix (keys sorted, every string quoted and escaped) and is the form the step-up challenge already uses.
**Would change if.** The independent review (D-032) prefers the length-prefixed form; a change needs a `format_version` bump and a re-encrypt of every row.

## D-048 Public pages are generated from `apps/web/pages`
**Status:** Proposed default (decided during the build, 2026-09-30).
**Context.** Phase 6 asks for pre-rendered public pages, and each legal document's version is the SHA-256 of its file (C-72), so a page rendered differently at every deploy would change its hash and look like a new version of the document.
**Choice.** Page bodies are fragments in `apps/web/pages`. `scripts/render-public-pages.mjs` wraps them in one shared header, footer and draft banner and writes complete static HTML to `apps/web/public`, deterministically (no dates, no build ids). The output is committed, and `npm run build` runs the renderer with `--check` and fails when it is stale. Today 13 pages come from fragments; the fee and commitments pages and five earlier legal documents are still hand-written in `apps/web/public`.
**Why.** A document's hash changes only when its words change, reviewers see the exact HTML in the diff, and the pages carry no script under the strict CSP.
**Would change if.** Counsel wants the documents delivered another way, or the hand-written pages move into fragments (which changes their hashes once).

**Context.** PLAN 4.3b puts the card host in its own project (`cards`) with no cookies and a read-only view of published cards. Keying that on `VERCEL_ENV` would also trip in any other project that runs the generator.

## D-051 Response headers follow PLAN 4.3a, with three CSP departures
**Status:** Proposed default (departs from the CSP row of PLAN 4.3a in three places; decided during the build, 2026-09-30).
**Context.** PLAN 4.3a sets one response-header set for every route of `web` and `cards` (D-015). The two `vercel.json` files had drifted from it: `Strict-Transport-Security` carried `includeSubDomains` (the plan adds it only after a subdomain audit), the web CSP lacked `worker-src`, `manifest-src` and `upgrade-insecure-requests`, `/boot.js` sent `max-age=0, must-revalidate` instead of `no-cache`, `/api/*` had no `Cache-Control` rule, and `cards` had its own `Permissions-Policy` (with the unknown `interest-cohort` token), `no-referrer`, and no `X-Frame-Options` or `Reporting-Endpoints`.
**Choice.** Both files now send the plan's value for every row, and the Report-Only row is merged into the enforced policy, as the plan says once the Nest ships. ST-14 (`e2e/headers.prod.spec.ts`, `e2e/headers.cards.spec.ts`) reads the table and the CSP row from `PLAN.md` and compares every served value on every static route class, so the files and the plan cannot drift apart again. Three departures remain, each because the plan's value breaks something that works: (1) web `img-src 'self' data: blob:` keeps `data:`, because the hatch card portrait is a PNG data URL (`apps/web/src/world/engine.ts`, shown by `CardPanel`; asserted in `e2e/account.spec.ts`); (2) web `trusted-types mosshatch` names the app's one policy, because `apps/web/src/lib/trusted.ts` creates it at start; (3) `cards` leaves out `script-src`, because hatchkind.com runs no script and its build gate (`scripts/check-cards.mjs`, ST-145) fails any CSP with `script-src`, so scripts fall back to `default-src 'none'`.
**Why.** With the plan's exact policy in Chromium, the app does not boot (`Policy "mosshatch" disallowed`) and a data-URL image is refused. The named list still refuses a `default` policy and a second `mosshatch` policy (ST-38). Known limits: on `cards`, `report-uri` and `report-to` name `/api/csp-report`, which the static card host does not serve, so its reports are dropped until it has a report function; the API function sets its own headers (`packages/api/src/http/router.ts`: `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `Cache-Control: no-store`, `Strict-Transport-Security` with `includeSubDomains`), which override `vercel.json` on API routes and were not changed here. Only Chromium was run, and how Vercel merges overlapping header rules is untested (4.3a).
**Would change if.** The card portrait moves to a `blob:` URL (drop `data:`), the app stops creating a Trusted Types policy (use the plan's empty `trusted-types`), or hatchkind.com ever ships script (it then takes `script-src 'self'`).

## D-053 CSP reports at `/api/csp-report`, kept 30 days
**Status:** Proposed default (the path is PLAN 4.3a's; the retention, the fields kept and the limits are own targets; decided during the build, 2026-09-30).
**Context.** PLAN 4.3a names `/api/csp-report` in `report-uri` and `Reporting-Endpoints` and says what the handler accepts, but gives no retention and no limits. Anyone can post to the endpoint, so every report is attacker-controlled.
**Choice.** `POST /api/csp-report` (anonymous, outside `/api/v1` as PLAN 4.5 lists it) accepts `application/csp-report` and `application/reports+json`, caps the body at 8 KiB, and allows 20 reports a minute per source network and 200 a minute overall (own targets). It keeps three coarse fields counted per day in `csp_reports` (migration 1100, no user id): the directive, the blocked resource's origin or a CSP keyword or scheme name, and the document path without its query or fragment. It drops reports caused by browser extensions and reports about documents on other origins, and logs nothing. The daily `csp.reports_sweep` job deletes rows older than 30 days (`packages/api/src/csp/report.ts`; `csp/csp.test.ts`, 10 tests).
**Why.** Enough to see that a violation happens and on which page, without storing a URL, a sample, a referrer, a user agent or an address. Thirty days matches the log retention in PLAN 4.3b (Operations: alerts, logs and on-call). The known limit that `cards` names the endpoint but cannot serve it is in D-051.
**Would change if.** You want a longer trend history, or reports go to an outside collector.

## D-054 An approved agent purchase expires 72 hours after approval
**Status:** Proposed default (PLAN 4.5 names no expiry for an approved request; decided during the build, 2026-09-30).
**Context.** PLAN 4.5 gives a proposal 72 hours. An approval whose order was never made (the order path failed after the approval committed, or nobody pressed Pay now) held its spend-cap reservation forever (`docs/PHASE5.md`, Round 3).
**Choice.** The approval expires `REQUEST_TTL_MS` (72 hours) after it was given (`APPROVAL_TTL_MS` in `packages/api/src/agents/requests.ts`). From then Pay now answers 409 `request_expired`; 10 minutes later the sweeper marks the request `expired` (`approval_expired`) and releases the reservation. It applies to purchases (`register`, `renew`) with no order only; an approval whose order exists is settled by that order (D-045). Pay now and the sweeper both take the request row lock, so an expired approval never yields an order (`agents/approval-expiry.test.ts`, `agents/order-expiry-race.test.ts`).
**Why.** It reuses the only figure the plan gives for agent requests instead of inventing one, and it is long enough to pay after a failed first attempt.
**Would change if.** You want the cap freed sooner (a shorter window) or approvals kept longer.

## D-055 Auto-renew mandates are kept 3 years after the last renewal charge

## D-056 Rate limits for the OAuth metadata fetch and authorize endpoints
**Status:** Proposed default (own targets; awaiting your sign-off, `docs/PHASE5.md` Decisions needed 3; decided during the build, 2026-09-30).
**Context.** The OAuth server fetches a Client ID Metadata Document from whatever URL a client names, so an anonymous caller can aim the fetcher; the authorize endpoint is anonymous too. The rate-limit table in PLAN 4.5 names neither.
**Choice.** Metadata-document fetches: 3 per URL and 30 per host per 10 minutes (`CIMD_FETCH_LIMITS`, `packages/api/src/oauth/clients.ts`). Authorize: 60 per source network (/24, or /48 for IPv6) per 10 minutes (`AUTHORIZE_LIMIT`, `oauth/authorize.ts`). The other two limits of the OAuth server are listed with these in the Phase 5 report: client registration 20 per source network per hour, and the token endpoint 120 a minute per source network and credential. Tests: `oauth/review.test.ts`.
**Why.** A URL is fetched a few times while a person consents, not dozens; the per-host limit bounds how hard the fetcher can be pointed at one host from many addresses; and keying authorize on the network rather than the address stops a caller from escaping the limit by rotating addresses within one network, while 60 in 10 minutes is far more than people consenting from one office need (own judgement, untested with real clients).
**Would change if.** Real clients hit the limits (none has connected yet), or you want different numbers.

## D-057 Link previews of hatchkind.com cards use the computed SVG
