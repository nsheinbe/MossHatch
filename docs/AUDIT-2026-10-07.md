# Product, purchase and fulfillment audit — 2026-10-07

Scope: mosshatch.com as served on 2026-10-07, the `main` branch it runs (815aa0a), the two Vercel projects, the production
database's operational state (flags, alerts and aggregate counts only; no personal data was read), Resend and the public DNS.
Method: live inspection first, then code, then local end-to-end runs (real API router, fake Stripe, mock registrar) on desktop
(1280×800) and mobile (390×844), plus four read-only specialist reviews (fulfillment, pricing, ownership/renewals, privacy).
Nothing was bought, charged, registered, transferred or emailed to a person. No production setting was changed.

Classification used below: **Defect** (confirmed, with evidence), **Gap** (missing capability), **Deliberate** (an owner decision),
**Unverified** (cannot be settled from here; says what would settle it).

## 1. Verdict

MossHatch is a well-engineered system whose sales path has **never run in production** and is **currently switched off by
accident**. Invite-only access is a deliberate owner decision (D-058) and should stay until the real-world gates below are closed;
removing the gate would not make purchases reliable. Before any invited purchase, the operator must clear an auto-safe pause that has
been on since 2026-10-03, and several fulfillment defects (fixed in this branch) could have voided a registration that actually
succeeded while telling the customer "Nothing was charged."

### Why sales are paused right now (root cause)

The first cron tick after cron was added (2026-10-03 14:38) raised an `audit.anchor_stale` **page**. Production's alert notifier is
`console.warn` (`packages/api/src/boot.ts:129`), so nobody saw it; `autoSafeMode` sets `registrar_writes_paused` after 30 minutes
without an acknowledgement (`packages/api/src/ops/alerts.ts:54`). The anchor alert later closed, but the flag is cleared only by an
operator SQL statement, so the pause has lasted four days. Any page (a Radar review, `job.dead`, the 36-hour alarm) will do the same
again until pages reach a person.

Worse, checkout did not check that flag: an invited buyer could open Checkout, have their card authorized, and the order would sit at
"Payment is on hold. Registering the name." until about 24 hours before the hold expired, then void (`orders/create.ts:87` checks only
`orders_paused`; `orders/machine.ts:383` waits on the flag).

## 8. The next three opportunities

1. **A transactional-mail outbox (F11).** Receipts, void notices and refund notices are sent after the database commit with no retry, so
   one failed send loses them for good. An outbox row written in the same transaction and a job that sends and retries would make every
   confirmation reach the customer, which matters most at the moment money and ownership change hands.
2. **Self-serve renewal recovery (O6, O7).** Renewing online during the grace period at a confirmed price, and replacing the saved
   auto-renew card before a charge fails, both still need support. Renewals carry most of the contribution (section 5), so this is
   where retention is won or lost.
3. **One Openprovider sandbox registration before the live rehearsal.** It settles the behaviours that are still unverified: WHOIS
   privacy as RDAP shows it, single-word registrant names under the claim rule (F12), whether transfers away are visible (F13), and
   whether a new zone carries default records. The checkout copy and the getting-started checklist could then state those facts outright.
